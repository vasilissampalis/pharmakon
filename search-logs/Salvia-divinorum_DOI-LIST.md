# Salvia divinorum / salvinorin A — evidence map and PDF list (v0.1, 2026-10-16)

All DOIs below come from Crossref API responses retrieved on 2026-10-16 (never from memory). Each row's DOI, title, container-title, year and first author were returned by Crossref for that record. Retraction check: none of the retained records is retracted; one carries a published corrigendum (item 8) and one a published erratum (item 46), both marked, and one retracted paper was found in the wider salvinorin literature and excluded (see Verification block). Save each PDF into `PDFs/Salvia-divinorum/` with the filename given.

**This is a thin literature and the list is correspondingly short (53 papers, against 70–110 for a Tier-1 substance).** The whole controlled human evidence base is seven studies from three groups (items 10–17), all small, all in healthy volunteers with prior hallucinogen experience, and five of them from a single Johns Hopkins / Yale / Barcelona cluster. There is one systematic review (item 9, published this year), no meta-analysis, no cohort study, no randomised therapeutic trial, and the entire dependence literature is one case series (item 19) plus the substance-use-disorder item in a national survey (item 20). Everything else is survey, case-report, phytochemistry or rodent work. The monograph should say this explicitly rather than implying a settled evidence base.

How this list was built: (1) 7 section-level and targeted Crossref bibliographic queries covering kappa-opioid pharmacology, the controlled human studies, pharmacokinetics, epidemiology, legal status, dependence and adverse effects; (2) the landmark studies with their published critiques and negative replications (Roth 2002 with the human studies that followed; Johnson 2011 and MacLean 2013 with Mendelson's null sublingual trial, which bears directly on route); (3) a topic-level Crossref `has-update:true` retraction and erratum screen. All query strings are in `Salvia-divinorum_search-log.md`. The PubMed layer (strings in the search log) is still to be run and will add to this list; its results decide what is added or dropped, not this list.

## Known gaps

**Controlled human evidence.** Items 10–17 are the whole of it. All are single-session, healthy-volunteer, hallucinogen-experienced samples of roughly 4–12 participants. No study has examined repeated administration, no study has included people who use Salvia divinorum regularly outside the laboratory, and no study has followed participants beyond the post-acute window of item 13. Any statement in the monograph about long-term effects, tolerance, or effects in people who are not hallucinogen-experienced has no human source and must say so.

**Dependence.** Item 19 is a case series and item 20 a cross-sectional survey item; item 48 is a monkey self-administration study finding salvinorin A to act as a punisher rather than a reinforcer. These point in opposite directions and the monograph's dependence section should present that as the contested question it is (METHODS §7) rather than resolving it.

**Not found at all, and probably non-existent:** any randomised therapeutic trial; any pregnancy data; any driving or psychomotor-impairment study; any harm-reduction intervention study; any mortality or poison-centre series specific to Salvia divinorum; any drug-checking data on the potency of the "fortified" 5x–60x extracts that dominate the retail market, which is the single most important unanswered practical question for the routes-of-administration section.

**Papers that exist and should probably be in the tables but were not surfaced in the rate-limited window:** Valdés, Díaz & Paul 1983 (J Ethnopharmacol, the ethnopharmacology of the Mazatec use of Salvia divinorum) and Valdés 1984 on the isolation of salvinorin A; Siebert's later route-of-administration work; Lange et al. 2008 (college survey preceding item 29); Hoover et al. 2008 on Salvia use among US adolescents; Travis & Lurie's legal-status surveys after item 36; Johnson et al.'s human abuse-potential assessment work later than item 16; and the Spanish-language *Salud Mental* review by Díaz 2014 (DOI 10.17711/sm.0185-3325.2014.022), which was surfaced but left out pending a decision on whether Spanish-language sources will be flagged and translated under METHODS §3.3.
