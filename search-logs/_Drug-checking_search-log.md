# _Drug-checking — search log (2026-10-26)

**Question** (the senior author, 26 October): are home drug-test kits useful? Should people use them, do they detect contaminants, and does testing change what people do or prevent overdoses?

**Priorities, in order:** (1) behaviour after a test result (throwing a drug away, taking less, not using alone, carrying naloxone, telling others); (2) overdose, hospital visits or deaths; (3) accuracy and false results beyond the five studies already read; (4) published cautions and critiques, for balance.

**How:** web searches for systematic reviews, trials and cohort studies of fentanyl, benzodiazepine, xylazine and nitazene test strips, reagent colour tests and drug checking services (to 2026), and for the named starting points; each chosen paper's Crossref record and OpenAlex open-access record fetched one at a time by an AI co-editor agent (fetch notes kept with the session's working files). Full texts were not read for selection; titles, journals and abstracts where Crossref had them were enough to choose.

**Starting points checked:** a commentary remembered as "Bardwell & Kerr 2018" is McGowan, Harris, Platt, Hope & Rhodes (2018); a JAMA Network Open study remembered as 2024 is Vickers-Smith et al. (2025), 8(5), e2510077. Left out as overlapping papers already listed: Peiper et al. 2019 (covered by the South Atlantic study and the scoping review), Tupper et al. 2018 (the same Vancouver service as Karamouzian et al. 2018), Goldman et al. 2019 (the qualitative companion of Krieger et al. 2018), Valente et al. 2019 (festival checking, covered by Measham 2019 and the systematic review) and a 2023 Harm Reduction Journal paper (10.1186/s12954-023-00767-0), a smaller version of Vickers-Smith et al.

**What the search did not find:** no published results of a randomized trial of fentanyl test strips, only trial protocols (10.1186/s13063-020-04898-8 and 10.1186/s13063-024-08440-y). The overdose evidence found is observational (one service) or state-level.

**Possible additions, not yet checked against Crossref:** a laboratory study of two strip brands against many synthetic opioids (10.1186/s12954-023-00911-w) and an Australian festival drug-checking pilot (10.1186/s12954-022-00708-3).
