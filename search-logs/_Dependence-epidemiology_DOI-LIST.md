# _Dependence-epidemiology — evidence map and PDF list (v0.1, 2026-10-16)

Cross-cutting topic: the base rates and the conceptual disputes that every monograph's §8 ("Dependence and patterns of use") rests on — the conditional probability of dependence by substance; remission and recovery without treatment; "maturing out"; the Vietnam veteran studies; Rat Park; the addiction-as-brain-disease debate with its published critiques and replies; DSM-5 use-disorder prevalence from NESARC-III; and the diagnostic-criteria changes that make prevalence figures non-comparable across editions. Save each PDF into `PDFs/_Cross-cutting/` with the filename given.

How this list was built: (1) Crossref bibliographic searches on the pre-registered section queries (`_Dependence-epidemiology_search-log.md`); (2) landmark studies retrieved with their published critiques and replies, as METHODS §2.3(3) requires — the *Lancet Psychiatry* brain-disease exchange (items 19–23) and the Vietnam-veteran appraisals (items 15–16) are the two clusters this topic turns on; (3) DOI verification by Crossref `works?filter=doi:` for three landmarks whose titles the bibliographic search could not match. Screened out: predatory-looking venues, conference abstracts, book reviews, news items, and the large gambling- and gaming-"addiction" literature returned by the natural-recovery query.

**Verification.** Every DOI below was either (a) returned verbatim by a Crossref `works?query.bibliographic=` search in this session with the title, journal, year and first author echoed in the table, or (b) verified by a Crossref `works?filter=doi:` fetch in this session (items 1, 2, 17, 18, 24, 25 — marked **V** in the table). No DOI in this list was written from memory without such a check.

**Language note (METHODS §9).** Titles are reproduced exactly as Crossref returns them, including "abuse", "addicts" and "addiction" where they appear in a title or an instrument name. The editors' own text uses "dependence", "substance use disorder" and "people who use drugs", and names the diagnostic instrument behind every prevalence figure (METHODS §10.2).

## Known gaps this list does not yet cover

The Crossref search endpoint was shared with other co-editor sessions and refused several requests with HTTP 429; nine of the thirteen pre-registered queries were executed (see the log). The following are known to exist and are **not** in the list because no verified DOI for them was obtained in this session. None should enter the reference library until its DOI is verified.

- **Lewis, "Addiction and the brain: development, not disease" (*Neuroethics* 2017).** The recalled DOI `10.1007/s12152-017-9293-z` was included in a Crossref multi-DOI filter request that the server silently dropped (it returned the whole corpus rather than the three named records), so the DOI is **unverified and deliberately excluded**. Items 63 and 64 are commentaries *on* this paper, so the protocol's rule against citing a critique without its target currently applies here. Verify and add it first.
- **Robins, Helzer & Davis, "Narcotic use in Southeast Asia and afterward" (*Archives of General Psychiatry* 1975)** — the original Vietnam cohort report. The recalled DOI was in the same dropped filter request. Items 11–14 cover the follow-ups but not the 1975 primary.
- **Rat Park critiques and replications.** Gage & Sumnall, "Rat Park: how a rat paradise changed the narrative of addiction" (*Addiction* 2019) and Petrie's failed replication (*Psychological Reports* 1996) were not returned by the Rat Park query, which produced mainly unrelated rodent morphine-self-administration pharmacology. Item 26 therefore currently stands **without its published critiques**, which the protocol does not permit for a landmark; this is the second-highest-priority gap.
- **Remission systematic reviews.** Calabria et al. (*Drug and Alcohol Review* 2010, prospective studies of remission from amphetamine, cannabis, cocaine or opioid dependence) and Fleury et al. (*European Archives of Psychiatry and Clinical Neuroscience* 2016, remission from substance use disorders: a systematic review and meta-analysis) were not returned; the natural-recovery query matched mainly on treatment and recovery-culture literature.
- **Long-term cohort follow-ups.** Hser et al. (*Archives of General Psychiatry* 2001, 33-year follow-up of narcotics addicts) and Vaillant's 20-year follow-up were not searched.
- **Earlier "maturing out" sources.** Winick (*Bulletin on Narcotics* 1962) has no DOI and must be obtained as a report; Sobell, Ellingstad & Sobell (*Addiction* 2000, natural recovery from alcohol and drug problems) was not returned.
- **Heyman's quantitative work.** *Addiction: A Disorder of Choice* (2009, book) and Heyman (*Frontiers in Psychiatry* 2013, "Quitting drugs: quantitative and qualitative features") — only the 2021 *BBS* commentary (item 78) was obtained.
- **ICD-11.** Poznyak et al. and Saunders et al. on substance use and addictive disorders in ICD-11 were not searched; R1 is listed as a report pending.
- **Published critiques of Volkow et al. 2016.** The correspondence in the *NEJM* following item 18 was not retrieved, so item 18 currently stands without the critiques the protocol requires.
- **GBD attributable burden** is covered in `_Harm-rankings_DOI-LIST.md` and is not duplicated here.
