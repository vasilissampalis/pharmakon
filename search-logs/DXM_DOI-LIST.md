# Dextromethorphan (DXM) — evidence map and PDF list (v0.1, 2026-10-16)

All DOIs below come from Crossref API responses retrieved on 2026-10-15/16 (never from memory). Each row's DOI, title, container-title, year and first author were returned by Crossref for that record. Save each PDF into `PDFs/DXM/` with the filename given; cross-cutting papers go in `PDFs/_Cross-cutting/`.

**This is a thin literature and the list is correspondingly short (40 papers, against 70–110 for a Tier-1 substance).** That is not an artefact of the search. Crossref bibliographic searching on dextromethorphan returns, over and over, the same three kinds of record: *Reactions Weekly* one-paragraph adverse-reaction abstracts (dozens of them, excluded), analytical-chemistry papers on quantifying dextromethorphan in cough syrup (excluded), and the pseudobulbar-affect trial literature for dextromethorphan/quinidine (retained selectively). There is no systematic review or meta-analysis of non-medical dextromethorphan use, no cohort study of people who use it, no controlled study of dependence or withdrawal, and no neuroimaging in people who use it. The strongest human evidence is four small laboratory studies, one dependence case series, and four poison-centre surveillance analyses.

How this list was built: (1) 12 section-level and targeted Crossref bibliographic queries covering pharmacokinetics and CYP2D6 phenotype, dextrorphan and NMDA pharmacology, serotonin toxicity with SSRIs and MAOIs, combination-product toxicity, poison-centre data, adolescent use, dependence and the therapeutic literature; (2) the landmark studies with their critiques and follow-ups; (3) a topic-level Crossref `has-update:true` retraction and erratum screen, which Crossref refused twice in this window (see "Verification block"). All query strings are in `DXM_search-log.md`. The PubMed layer (strings in the search log) is still to be run and will add to this list; its results decide what is added or dropped, not this list.

## Known gaps

The gaps here are of two kinds. The first is papers that exist and that this list should contain, but whose DOIs Crossref did not surface inside a heavily rate-limited window: Reissig et al. 2012 (Psychopharmacology, "High doses of dextromethorphan, an NMDA antagonist, produce effects similar to classic hallucinogens" — the single most important human laboratory study for this monograph, and the direct predecessor of items 6 and 7); Carbonaro et al. 2018 (the companion analysis of the psilocybin/dextromethorphan comparison); Chyka et al. 2007 (Clin Toxicol, the AAPCC evidence-based consensus guideline for out-of-hospital management of dextromethorphan poisoning); Romanelli & Smith 2009 (J Am Pharm Assoc, clinical effects and management); Boyer 2004 (Pediatr Emerg Care) and Schwartz 2005 (Clin Pediatr) on adolescent use; Logan's forensic series on dextromethorphan-related deaths and driving-under-the-influence cases (J Anal Toxicol / J Forensic Sci); Murray & Brewerton 1993 (South Med J) on over-the-counter use by teenagers; Iosifescu et al. 2022 (J Clin Psychiatry, the GEMINI trial of dextromethorphan–bupropion in major depression) and the ASCEND trial, which the therapeutic section needs alongside items 31–34; Nguyen et al. on sigma-1 receptor pharmacology. Each should be found by the PubMed strings P1–P12 in the search log.

The second kind is evidence that appears not to exist. No systematic review or meta-analysis of non-medical dextromethorphan use was found. No cohort or longitudinal study of people who use dextromethorphan was found; the long-term outcome literature is a single dependence and withdrawal case series (item 8). No controlled study of dextromethorphan withdrawal, no neuroimaging study in people who use it, no study of pregnancy outcomes, no harm-reduction intervention study, and no drug-checking or adulterant data were found. The combination-product question that the monograph most needs — how much of the harm in intentional high-dose use comes from the chlorpheniramine and acetaminophen in the product rather than from dextromethorphan — is addressed only indirectly, by items 9, 10 and 20; no study was found that separates the contributions. Greek data: none.
