# General — evidence map and PDF list for general facts about drug use (v0.1, 2026-10-31)

Cross-cutting topic requested by the senior author: quiz questions and answers about drug use in general ("like men vs women, telescoping and all the important general facts for drugs that users would find interesting to know"), each with "a critical scientific objective criticism". Every quiz answer must rest on checked evidence, so this list gathers, for each of the eleven questions of the brief, the backbone reviews, the landmark studies and their published critiques: (1) women and men; (2) how many people who use a drug develop dependence, and how many recover; (3) age at first use; (4) the "gateway" idea and its alternatives; (5) using several drugs; (6) tolerance and overdose after a break; (7) expectations and placebo; (8) environment and addiction; (9) heritability; (10) how harms are ranked; (11) words and stigma. Out of scope: dose amounts, timing, routes, mixing, drug checking, how deaths are counted, and anything about making effects stronger. Reading list only: no claims, no quiz items, no site text. Brief: `docs/TOPIC-MAP-BRIEF-2026-10-31b.md`.

How this list was built: PubMed through the E-utilities interface (`esearch`, `esummary`; `efetch` abstracts where a title did not show the design) in a tab of the app's browser that this reader opened, 2–8 searches per question plus known-item checks (strings, hit counts and what was kept: `General_search-log.md`). Order of preference: systematic reviews and meta-analyses; large population studies and authoritative reviews; landmark studies with their published critiques (METHODS §2.3(3)); animal studies only for the questions that are about animal experiments (Rat Park and choice studies), marked as such. The cross-cutting lists that overlap this topic were checked first (`_Dependence-epidemiology`, `_Harm-rankings`, `_Language-and-stigma`, `_Tolerance`, `_Harm-reduction`, `_Overdose-and-emergency`) and all other `search-logs/*_DOI-LIST.md`: papers already on them are named in each section's "Already on other lists" line instead of being listed again, and several of the gaps those lists name are filled here. Papers already in the reference library (`data/references/*.json`) are in the table "Already in the library" at the end, not in the main list.

**Columns.** **DOI**: every DOI was fetched from Crossref `works/<DOI>` in the browser tab on 2026-10-31 and matched to the PubMed record on title, year and first author; its `updated-by` field was read (corrections, retractions). Where Crossref's year (online) and PubMed's year (print issue) differ, the print year is given with the online year in brackets. **Free?**: `PMCxxxxxxx` = free full text in PubMed Central (from the PubMed record); `publisher` = the Crossref record carries a Creative Commons licence for the published version (open at the publisher); `—` = neither. OpenAlex was not used in this run (its rules allow PubMed E-utilities and Crossref `works/<DOI>` only), so some articles free at the publisher without a licence in Crossref show `—`. Free papers can be read by AI readers without the senior author saving them.

**Tiers.** A = read first: the backbone review or best study for each question, each landmark study and its main published critique. B = further studies, reviews and critiques.

Save each PDF under `PDFs/` with the path given (drug folder from `docs/TIER1.md` for papers about one drug, or `_Cross-cutting/` for papers that span several drugs).

**Already on other lists (not repeated):** Alcohol #42 Frezza et al. 1990 (high blood alcohol levels in women: gastric alcohol dehydrogenase and first-pass metabolism; landmark, read with Ammon 1996 below) and _Dependence-epidemiology #6 Wagner & Anthony 2007 (sex/gender differences in progression to dependence on cannabis, cocaine and alcohol), Tier A for this section; #38 Kerridge et al. 2018 and #39 Kerridge et al. 2015 (gender-specific DSM-5 profiles) and #42 Agrawal & Lynskey 2007 (gender and criteria for cannabis use disorders), Tier B.

**Known gaps.** Telescoping was described in treatment samples (Randall 1999; Hernandez-Avila 2004). The general-population tests found little or no evidence of it (Keyes 2010, two US national surveys, where men had the shorter time to dependence in younger cohorts; Alvanzo 2011, no telescoping in any race/ethnicity group), and a not-in-treatment heroin sample found none from first to intensive use (Stoltman 2015), as their abstracts state. Higher blood alcohol in women after the same dose is usually attributed to body water and size; the stomach-enzyme explanation of Frezza 1990 (Alcohol #42) was contested by Ammon 1996 (first-pass metabolism small in both sexes), and the later review (Baraona 2001) has no DOI. For other drugs, sex differences in effects rest on small laboratory studies (one each for MDMA and oral THC here; small cocaine and methamphetamine studies were not listed). Gender (social roles, access to treatment, childcare, stigma) and sex (biology) are hard to separate in these designs; nothing was found on transgender or non-binary people.

**Already on other lists (not repeated):** None of the gateway papers is on another list. Related: the age-at-first-use studies in section 3 and the heritability studies in section 9 (shared liability).

**Known gaps.** The "gateway" order (alcohol and tobacco, then cannabis, then other drugs) is well described in US cohorts; the cross-national surveys (Degenhardt 2010) show that the order varies between countries. Mendelian randomization (Reed 2022) relies on genetic instruments whose assumptions are themselves debated. The molecular "priming" evidence (Kandel & Kandel 2014) comes from mice. These searches found no study that separates the three explanations in one design.
