# GHB-GBL — evidence map and PDF list (v0.1, 2026-10-15)

All DOIs below were returned verbatim by Crossref in this session (Crossref `works?query.bibliographic=` records, which carry the same metadata and the same `update`/`updated-by` fields as the `works/<DOI>` endpoint). No DOI in this list was written from memory. Save each PDF into `PDFs/GHB-GBL/` with the filename given; cross-cutting papers go in `PDFs/_Cross-cutting/`.

How this list was built: (1) 10 pre-specified section queries covering the monograph sections of METHODS §8 plus the substance-specific topics (the steep dose–response; overdose and coma series; the withdrawal syndrome and its management; GBL and 1,4-butanediol as precursors; chemsex; dependence and detoxification by titration and tapering; baclofen and benzodiazepine treatment; endogenous GHB and the interpretation of post-mortem concentrations); (2) landmark studies with the published critiques and replies attached to them; (3) screening by title and journal.

This is a **mid-sized literature with a distinctive shape**: there are almost no randomised controlled trials of anything except sodium oxybate for narcolepsy, and the evidence base for GHB dependence and withdrawal consists of case series, uncontrolled cohorts and one national monitoring programme (the Dutch GHB Monitor). The list reflects that: case series are included where they are the best available evidence, and are marked as such so the study cards record the design honestly (METHODS §5.2).

Crossref throttled this session very heavily (HTTP 429 after roughly every second request, with pauses of 90–240 s between requests as the brief requires; direct non-WebFetch access to the API is blocked in this environment). 10 of ~14 planned queries returned records. Landmarks that could not be reached are listed by title under "Known gaps". The PubMed layer (strings in `GHB-GBL_search-log.md`) is still to be run and will add to this list; its results decide what is added or dropped, not this list.

## Known gaps this list does not yet cover (to come from the PubMed layer)

(Emerg Med J 2011); Liakoni et al. (Switzerland). **Deaths:** Zvosec et al., case series of GHB-related deaths (Am J Emerg Med 2011); Kim et al., GHB-related deaths in Los Angeles County; Andresen et al., GHB in forensic cases. **Dependence:** van Amsterdam et al. on GHB dependence and its treatment in the Netherlands; Brunt et al. on GHB dependence severity; Beurmanjer et al. on GHB use disorder outcomes and on cognitive function in GHB-dependent patients. **Neuroimaging and cognition:** Raposo Pereira et al.'s companion papers on grey matter and on the affective network (the two European Neuropsychopharmacology records found are conference abstracts, not the full papers); Bosch et al. on GHB-induced comas and cognition. **Pharmacology:** the GHB receptor versus GABA-B receptor literature (Wu, Castelli, Maitre); GHB transporter (MCT) kinetics and the non-linear pharmacokinetics that produce the steep dose–response in humans. **Interactions:** controlled human studies of GHB combined with ethanol (none located; the interaction evidence here is animal and case-report level, which the monograph must say). **Not covered at all:** GHB and driving; GHB in pregnancy and lactation (beyond the sodium-oxybate breast-milk abstract found); GHB use among women outside the sexual-assault literature; Greek GHB epidemiology beyond #73; detection windows for drug-checking services; 1,4-BD industrial exposure.

- Literature size note: this is a genuinely smaller and lower-design-quality literature than alcohol, cannabis or benzodiazepines. Outside sodium-oxybate narcolepsy research there are essentially no randomised trials, and the dependence/withdrawal evidence rests on case series and one national monitoring cohort. 84 papers is close to the practical ceiling of the peer-reviewed GHB/GBL literature that is worth a study card.
