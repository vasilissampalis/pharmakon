# Kratom (Mitragyna speciosa) — evidence map and PDF list (v0.1, 2026-10-16)

Scope: mitragynine and 7-hydroxymitragynine pharmacology, mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, human pharmacokinetics, use patterns and dependence, withdrawal, self-treatment of opioid withdrawal, deaths and poison-centre reports with the interpretation debates, liver injury, concentrated 7-OH and extract products, and the Southeast Asian traditional-use literature.

**This is a thinner literature than the other four opioid maps.** There are no randomised trials of kratom in humans, no cohort studies of long-term outcomes, and the mortality evidence rests on case series and medical-examiner data in which kratom is almost always one of several substances detected. The list is accordingly smaller (72 papers) than the 90–120 of a mature Tier-1 substance, and the "Known gaps" section is correspondingly longer. Where the field's own methodologists have said the evidence cannot bear the weight placed on it (Henningfield; Smith; Papsun), those papers are in Tier A so the monograph inherits the caveats with the claims.

Every DOI below was taken verbatim from a Crossref record returned in this session. No DOI was written from memory. Save each PDF into `PDFs/Kratom/` with the filename given.

Verification codes: **CS** = Crossref search record returned in this session. No `works/<DOI>` re-fetches were needed. The `updated-by` field was not read, so the Retraction Watch check of METHODS §4.3 is still required at study-card stage; one correction was identified from the search records and is listed with its parent paper.

## Known gaps this list does not yet cover

- **Kruegel's synthetic and receptor-pharmacology series** — Kruegel et al. 2016 (J Am Chem Soc, synthetic and receptor signalling studies of the Mitragyna alkaloids), Kruegel et al. 2019 (J Med Chem / ACS Cent Sci, 7-hydroxymitragynine as the principal active metabolite) and Váradi et al. 2016 (J Med Chem, mitragynine pseudoindoxyl as a G-protein-biased agonist). Two targeted strings for these were refused or errored. **These are the papers the entire "biased agonism, less respiratory depression" claim rests on and must be obtained before that claim is written.** The three general biased-agonism papers in Tier C (items 65–67) are the critical counterweight, not a substitute.
- **Kratom respiratory-depression data in humans** — there is, to this list's knowledge, no controlled human study; the claim that mitragynine causes less respiratory depression than classical opioids rests on rodent work. The monograph should say so.
- **Controlled human laboratory studies**: the recent self-administration and subjective-effects work (McCurdy, Sharma and colleagues; the NIDA IRP human laboratory studies) was not surfaced; so was the pharmacokinetics work of Tanna, Paine and colleagues on mitragynine drug–drug interactions and CYP inhibition.
- **Poison-centre series**: Post et al. 2019 (Clin Toxicol, National Poison Data System kratom exposures), Eggleston et al. 2019 (Pharmacotherapy), Anwar et al. (MMWR). The poison-centre string was rate-limited.
- **CDC overdose-death analyses**: Olsen et al. 2019 (MMWR, kratom-involved overdose deaths) and the debates over how many of those deaths are attributable to kratom rather than co-detected substances.
- **Liver injury beyond Schimmel 2020**: the DILIN case series (Ahmad et al.) and the subsequent case reports and reviews.
- **Neonatal opioid withdrawal after maternal kratom use** — one non-indexed case series was surfaced but not retained; the published case series need locating.
- **Kratom and seizures, and kratom combined with other substances** (the "krypton" O-desmethyltramadol-adulterated product deaths in Sweden, Kronstrand et al. 2011).
- **Thai literature and the 2021 decriminalisation evaluation**, and the older Thai pharmacology and ethnobotany; most of the Southeast Asian evidence in this list is Malaysian and from one research group, which is itself a limitation the monograph should state.
- **Prevalence**: there is no satisfactory prevalence estimate (see item 12); NSDUH kratom items from 2021 onward are the best available and are a report, not a paper.
