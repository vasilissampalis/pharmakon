# Caffeine — evidence map and PDF list (v0.1, 2026-10-15)

All DOIs below were returned verbatim by Crossref in this session (Crossref `works?query.bibliographic=` records, which carry the same metadata and the same `update`/`updated-by` fields as the `works/<DOI>` endpoint). No DOI in this list was written from memory. Save each PDF into `PDFs/Caffeine/` with the filename given; cross-cutting papers go in `PDFs/_Cross-cutting/`.

How this list was built: (1) 10 pre-specified section queries covering the monograph sections of METHODS §8 plus the substance-specific topics (pharmacokinetics and CYP1A2; cognition and the withdrawal-reversal debate; sleep; dependence, withdrawal and the DSM-5 caffeine-use-disorder question; cardiovascular safety in large cohorts and in Mendelian randomisation; energy drinks mixed with alcohol; caffeine toxicity and deaths; pregnancy); (2) landmark studies together with the published critiques and replies attached to them — this literature is unusually rich in those, and three complete exchanges are listed (James/Rogers vs Smith on withdrawal reversal; Aubin on Crippa's mortality meta-analysis; Soltani on Jin & Qiao's pregnancy meta-analysis, plus Bracken on Chen's); (3) screening by title and journal.

Two features of this literature shape the list. First, **the confound is the finding**: almost every cognitive claim about caffeine is contested on withdrawal-reversal grounds, and almost every observational cardiovascular or pregnancy claim is contested on residual-confounding (especially smoking) grounds. The debate papers are therefore in Tier A, not in a footnote. Second, the **observational-to-genetic contrast** is the main way the field adjudicates causation, so the Mendelian-randomisation layer sits beside the cohort layer rather than after it.

Crossref throttled this session very heavily (HTTP 429 after roughly every second request, with pauses of 90–300 s between requests as the brief requires; direct non-WebFetch access to the API is blocked in this environment). 10 of ~14 planned queries returned records. Landmarks that could not be reached are listed by title under "Known gaps". The PubMed layer (strings in `Caffeine_search-log.md`) is still to be run and will add to this list; its results decide what is added or dropped, not this list.

**Work through the list in order. Tier A first. Tell me each time you finish a batch of ten and I will start reading.**

O'Keefe et al. (JACC 2013); Lopez-Garcia and Freedman cohort papers; Shah et al. on energy drinks and QT/blood pressure (J Am Heart Assoc 2019); Higgins on energy-drink cardiovascular effects. **Anxiety and psychiatry:** Klevebrant & Frick, caffeine and anxiety meta-analysis (Gen Hosp Psychiatry 2022); Charney and Bruce on caffeine-induced panic. **Therapeutic research:** Schmidt et al., the Caffeine for Apnea of Prematurity (CAP) trial and its 5- and 11-year follow-ups (NEJM 2006, 2007; JAMA 2012) — caffeine's single largest randomised evidence base and a required part of §12; caffeine citrate Cochrane reviews. **Performance:** Grgic et al.'s umbrella review of caffeine and exercise performance; the International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand. **Toxicity:** the caffeine-toxicity literature is thinly represented here — only #25, #62 and #78 were reachable, and the Crossref ranking for overdose terms was dominated by *Reactions Weekly* case notes.
