# DMT and ayahuasca — evidence map and PDF list (v0.1, 2026-10-16)

All DOIs below come from Crossref API responses retrieved on 2026-10-15/16 (never from memory). Each row's DOI, title, journal and year were returned by Crossref for that record. The DOI marked ◆ was additionally fetched at `works/<DOI>` and the `updated-by` field checked (retraction/erratum); the remaining rows come from Crossref bibliographic *search* responses in the same session. Retraction check: none retracted; no erratum found on any retained item. Save each PDF into `PDFs/DMT-Ayahuasca/` with the filename given; cross-cutting papers go in `PDFs/_Cross-cutting/`.

How this list was built: (1) the Strassman human-laboratory series and the Riba/Barcelona ayahuasca pharmacology series, which carry the pharmacology, pharmacokinetics and acute-effects sections; (2) the ayahuasca depression RCT with the observational cohorts and surveys that qualify it; (3) the Global Ayahuasca Survey adverse-event work and its reanalysis; (4) the modern intravenous and extended-state DMT studies; (5) section-level Crossref searches (strings in `DMT-Ayahuasca_search-log.md`), screened by title and journal — Reactions Weekly items, conference abstracts and preclinical-only papers dropped except where no human data exist.

Context note for the senior author: Crossref refusal rates rose through the build (roughly two calls in three were refused by the end). Three planned topics were not reached and are listed under "Known gaps". The list is accordingly shorter than a full Tier-1 map; the PubMed layer should close most of it.

**Work through the list in order. Tier A first.**

## Known gaps this list does not yet cover

Planned queries that could not be run before Crossref refusals ended the window, and which the PubMed layer must cover: **long-term ayahuasca users** (Bouso et al. 2012 PLoS ONE on personality and neuropsychology in regular users; Bouso et al. 2015 on cortical thickness; Barbosa et al. on religious cohorts; Doering-Silveira et al. on adolescents in ayahuasca churches; Grob et al. 1996, the Hoasca Project); **inhaled and vaporised DMT pharmacokinetics** (Eckernäs et al.) — the list currently covers intravenous DMT only (items 14–16), so the route-of-administration section has no verified inhaled-route source; **the extended-state DMT trial itself** (Luan et al. 2024, J Psychopharmacol) — only the modelling paper (item 18) is verified; **Timmermann et al. 2018–2019** (DMT models the near-death experience; multivariate EEG).

Also missing with verified DOIs: Soler et al.'s ayahuasca-and-mindfulness study — only its **corrigendum** record surfaced (10.3389/fphar.2020.635111, Front Pharmacol 2021, "Corrigendum: Four weekly ayahuasca sessions lead to increases in 'acceptance' capacities"), not the parent article; obtain both by title. Sanches et al. 2016 (J Clin Psychopharmacol, open-label ayahuasca in depression) and Osório et al. 2015 (Braz J Psychiatry, preliminary report) failed verification in a batch request that returned a client error.

Sections still thin: **deaths and fatal toxicity** — nothing identified beyond the single intoxication description (item 42) and the serotonin-syndrome case (item 43); ayahuasca-related deaths in retreat settings are reported in the press but no peer-reviewed series was surfaced. **Dependence and withdrawal**: no source; the literature is thin by nature. **Pregnancy**: no human data identified. **Driving**: none. **Synthetic DMT analogues** (DiPT, 4-HO-MiPT, 5-MeO-MiPT) are covered only through item 48. **Greek data**: none.
