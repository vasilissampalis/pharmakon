# Heroin (diacetylmorphine) — evidence map and PDF list (v0.1, 2026-10-15)

Every DOI below was taken verbatim from a Crossref record returned in this session (search endpoint, `query.bibliographic`, `filter=type:journal-article`). No DOI was written from memory, so no DOI in this list is unverified; the verification code is given per paper in the last column. Save each PDF into `PDFs/Heroin/` with the filename given; papers marked `_Cross-cutting/` go in that folder instead and serve several opioid monographs.

How this list was built: (1) section-level Crossref bibliographic searches covering the monograph sections of METHODS §8 (pharmacology and pharmacokinetics; acute toxicity and overdose; dependence and natural history; long-term outcomes; epidemiology and mortality; harm-reduction interventions; therapeutic trials; routes of administration); (2) targeted author/title searches for the landmark studies this field relies on (Darke on fatal overdose; Hser on natural history; Degenhardt and Mathers on mortality; Strang and Oviedo-Joekes on heroin-assisted treatment; Clark on naloxone distribution) together with their published critiques and replies; (3) screening by title and journal, dropping conference abstracts, supplements, news items, trade newsletters and off-topic hits. Query strings and counts are in `Heroin_search-log.md`. The PubMed layer has not been run; its results decide what is added or dropped, not this list.

Verification codes: **CS** = Crossref search record (DOI, title, container-title and year returned by the Crossref search endpoint in this session). No `works/<DOI>` re-fetches were needed because no DOI was recalled from memory; the `updated-by` field was therefore not read for most records and the Retraction Watch check of METHODS §4.3 is still required at study-card stage. Two published corrections were nevertheless identified from the search records themselves and are listed with their parent papers.

**Work through the list in order. Tier A first. Tell me each time you finish a batch of ten.**

## Known gaps this list does not yet cover

The following are known landmarks or essential sources for this monograph whose DOIs were **not** surfaced by the Crossref searches that completed in this session (roughly half of the attempted calls were refused by the shared egress proxy — see the search log). None has been written from memory; each must be located and verified before use, and the PubMed layer is the natural place to do it.

- ~~**Sordo et al. 2017, BMJ**~~ — **resolved.** Four attempts during the Heroin build were refused or returned other records; a fifth string, run during the Methadone-Buprenorphine build, located it (10.1136/bmj.j1550). It is now item 35a, with the BMJ editorial on it (35b) and Cornish et al. 2010 (35c).
- **Strang et al. 2015, Br J Psychiatry** — "Heroin on trial: systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised trials of diamorphine-prescribing as treatment for refractory heroin addiction", and the Cochrane review of heroin maintenance (Ferri, Davoli & Perucci).
- **Oviedo-Joekes et al. 2016, JAMA Psychiatry** — SALOME main results (hydromorphone compared with diacetylmorphine); also the Swiss PROVE cohort (Perneger 1998 BMJ; Rehm 2001 Lancet) and the German and Dutch heroin trials (Haasen 2007; van den Brink 2003 BMJ).
- **Mechanisms of fatal opioid overdose**: White & Irvine 1999 (Addiction); Dahan et al. on opioid-induced respiratory depression and on the limits of naloxone reversal (five attempts at this topic were refused or errored); chest-wall rigidity literature (covered under Fentanyl).
- **Naloxone programme outcomes**: Walley et al. 2013 (BMJ, Massachusetts OEND); McDonald & Strang 2016 (Addiction, Bradford Hill appraisal); Bird et al. 2016 (Scotland National Naloxone Programme); the N-ALIVE pilot results; McDonald et al. on intranasal naloxone pharmacokinetics.
- **Supervised consumption facilities**: Marshall et al. 2011 (Lancet, Insite and overdose mortality) **and its published critiques**, Potier et al. 2014 (Drug Alcohol Depend systematic review), Milloy & Wood commentary, and the econometric critiques of the Insite evaluations.
- **Heroin pharmacokinetics in humans**: Rook et al. 2006 (Curr Clin Pharmacol / Pharm Res, intravenous and inhaled heroin PK in a treatment population) and Jenkins et al. 1994 (smoked and intravenous heroin PK).
- **Natural history and remission**: the Vietnam veteran studies (Robins 1974/1993) and the critiques of the Civil Addict Program cohort design; Calabria et al. 2010 on remission rates.
- **Global epidemiology**: Degenhardt et al. 2014 (Addiction, global burden of opioid dependence), Degenhardt et al. 2017 (Lancet Glob Health, global prevalence of injecting drug use), Larney et al. 2017 (Lancet Glob Health, global HCV/HIV among PWID), Colledge-Frisby et al. 2023 (Lancet Glob Health, harm-reduction coverage).
- **Infectious and injecting-related complications**: wound botulism outbreaks, injection-site infections and infective endocarditis cohorts; anthrax among people who inject heroin in Scotland (2009–10).
- **Pregnancy**: heroin use in pregnancy and neonatal outcomes — handled in the Methadone-Buprenorphine map (MOTHER trial and NAS literature); a heroin-specific pass is still owed.
- **Opioid cognition**: Wollman et al. on neuroimaging in opioid use disorder; structural and functional MRI meta-analyses specific to heroin.
- **Drug checking and the fentanyl-adulterated supply as it affects heroin markets** — covered in the Fentanyl-and-analogues map; the heroin-specific supply papers (Ciccarone on the heroin/fentanyl "triple wave"; Mars et al. on heroin source-form) were not surfaced.
