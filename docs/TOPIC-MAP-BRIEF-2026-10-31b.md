## What to map (each with the main critiques of the landmark papers)

- **Women and men:** differences in use, in how fast problems develop ("telescoping") and whether telescoping holds in
  general-population samples or only in treatment samples; differences in how the body handles alcohol and other drugs;
  differences in treatment access and outcomes.
- **How many people who use a drug develop dependence**, by drug, and how many recover, with and without treatment
  ("natural recovery", "maturing out"); the Vietnam veterans' heroin follow-up and its critiques.
- **Age at first use** and later problems: association against cause (shared risk factors).
- **The "gateway" idea** and the alternatives (common liability, availability), with the strongest tests of each.
- **Using several drugs** as the usual pattern; what that does to studies that blame one drug.
- **Tolerance and overdose after a break** (release from prison, leaving treatment or hospital): how much the risk rises
  and for how long.
- **Expectations and placebo** in drug effects (for example alcohol placebo studies, "set").
- **Environment and addiction**: "Rat Park" and its replications, animal choice studies with alternatives, human choice
  studies (alternatives to the drug), and what they can and cannot show.
- **Heritability** of addiction from twin and adoption studies, and what a heritability figure does and does not mean.
- **How harms are ranked** (for example multi-criteria rankings of drug harms) and their critiques.
- **Words and stigma**: studies of how labels ("abuser" against "person with a substance use disorder") change judgments
  by clinicians and the public.

Out of scope: dose amounts, timing, routes, mixing (the interactions page has it), drug checking, how deaths are counted
(its own map), and anything about making effects stronger. No claims, cards or site text: lists and logs only.

Final message under 200 words: counts per tier, DOIs verified and dropped, corrections or retractions found, gaps.
