## 1. The Indian lists

Under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985 (NDPS Act), there are five lists:

- **Narcotic drugs.** These are defined in the Act itself:
  - s. 2(iii) cannabis (hemp);
  - s. 2(v) coca derivative;
  - s. 2(vi) coca leaf;
  - s. 2(xvi) opium derivative;
  - s. 2(xi) manufactured drug;
  - s. 2(xiv) narcotic drug.

  Further "manufactured drugs" are declared by notification under s. 2(xi)(b). The base notification is S.O. 826(E) of 14 Nov 1985, extended by S.O. 40(E) of 29 Jan 1993 and later notifications.
- **Psychotropic substances.** These are the substances in the Schedule to the Act (s. 2(xxiii)), amended by notifications under s. 3.
- **NDPS Rules, 1985, Schedule I** (see rules 53 and 64). It has Part I (narcotic drugs) and Part II (psychotropic substances).
- **Essential narcotic drugs.** Declared under s. 2(viiia) by S.O. 1181(E) of 5 May 2015, "for medical and scientific use".
- **Controlled substances (precursors).** Listed in the NDPS (Regulation of Controlled Substances) Order, 2013, Schedules A to C.

The government body in charge is the Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance. Its Central Bureau of Narcotics (CBN) publishes the lists. All texts are bilingual Hindi and English; the English text was read.

## 4. Method of reading

- **Two readings.** Every list or notification was read twice through WebFetch, with differently worded prompts and output formats ("N | name | other | status" versus "N ;; name ;; other ;; status"). The CBN list was read in four chunks: psychotropics 1-55 and 56-111, natural plus manufactured 1-52, and manufactured 53-105.
- **Script comparison.** The two readings were compared by Python scripts. Both readings were pasted verbatim into each script as inline input.
  - Psychotropic rows: compared on normalised name and other-name text.
  - Manufactured rows: the short reading was tested as a prefix or substring of the full reading.
  - Notifications and definitions: compared on serial and name.
  - No reading files were saved: the caller asked for only the two output files.
- **Matching.** The 77 items were matched by script against all 364 entries read, using name variants as search keys. Every hit was then checked by eye.
