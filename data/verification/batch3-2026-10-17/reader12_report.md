## Papers

**Srisurapanont 2021 (network meta-analysis; AMSTAR 2 low confidence).** The review found six head-to-head randomized trials (395 people, 21 to 42 days, Thailand, Iran and China, mostly inpatients). None had a placebo arm, and each trial found no significant difference between its two drugs. The network gives indirect advantages for olanzapine (−1.09, −1.89 to −0.28) and quetiapine (−0.86) over risperidone, and for four drugs over aripiprazole, all rated low certainty. The dropout intervals are uninformative (for example 0.00 to 218). Main limit, from the editors' arithmetic: the network is a chain with no closed loop, so the indirect estimates are just sums of single-trial estimates. Both advantages over risperidone pass through one open-label pilot trial of haloperidol against risperidone (44 people). The abstract's "preferred antipsychotic" rests entirely on these indirect chains. Critical item 11 was judged not met; if it were judged met, the rating would be moderate confidence.

**Voce 2019 (systematic review, no meta-analysis; AMSTAR 2 critically low).** The review covers 94 studies and 7387 people, mostly hospital samples. Across 27 studies with duration data, the median study had 25% still psychotic more than a month after stopping (range 0% to 96%). The median was 40% in longitudinal studies, and 44% in Japanese studies against 12% elsewhere; the authors explain this by the ICD's 6-month rule. Later schizophrenia diagnoses were 33–38% after 6–7 years in two studies and 16% within 16 years in a Finnish register study. Limits: no protocol, no quality appraisal, single extraction, and unweighted medians that include six laboratory studies where persistence was impossible by design.

**Cohen-Laroque 2024 (meta-analysis; AMSTAR 2 critically low).** The review pools 12 studies (624 people with methamphetamine psychosis, 527 with schizophrenia). Negative symptoms were milder in methamphetamine psychosis (−0.35, −0.54 to −0.16; I² 54%), and this can be reproduced from the review's own data. The positive-symptom null (−0.01, −0.13 to 0.11; I² 0%) **cannot be reproduced**: Table 1's means and SDs give −0.29 (−0.67 to 0.10), I² 89% (editors' recalculation), and none of the 12 study values in the forest plot matches the table. Other problems: groups depart from the review's own eligibility criteria (a comparison group that used methamphetamine; a first-episode comparison group), and two Taiwanese studies probably double-count one sample.

**Boden 2023 (general-population birth cohort, New Zealand; ROBINS-E high overall, from concerns in five domains; a second reader may fairly say some concerns).** Any use in a period went with self-reported psychotic symptoms in the same period: OR 1.88 unadjusted, 1.33 (1.03–1.72) after adjustment that included concurrent cannabis use. The higher odds were concentrated in the 5% who had ever used at least weekly (2.85, 1.21–6.69). That 2.85 is adjusted for fixed confounders only, although the abstract reports it as if it carried the full adjustment. Limits: the outcome is psychotic-like experiences with high base rates (35–42% to age 25); use and symptoms were reported for the same periods; the E-value is 1.57.

## Reporting errors that matter
- Cohen-Laroque: forest-plot values for positive symptoms do not match Table 1; Mikami 2003's negative-symptom value does not match Table 1 (−1.05 vs about −2.1); Table 1 labels as SD what are SEs for Dawe 2011 and Hajebi 2018; three studies were analysed with group sizes different from Table 1; the abstract's p values are heterogeneity p values; 524 vs 527; the PROSPERO number's year (2021) does not match the stated date (2020).
- Srisurapanont: the text prints quetiapine as +0.86 inside a CI of −1.61 to −0.11; Section 3.2 says paliperidone vs aripiprazole (it was risperidone); record counts differ between text and Fig. 1 (775/641 vs 1,120/841; 841 − 830 ≠ 10); 5 vs 4 trials for positive symptoms; ages in the text do not match Table 1.
- Voce: the abstract says 20 longitudinal studies (the results say 10); the substance categories sum to 112 of 94 studies; 8/24 is printed as 36%; transition sample sizes are written with k.
- Boden: the 2.85 is adjusted for fixed confounders only; Model 2 vs Model 3 labels; "incidence" at 30–35 is a period prevalence; child IQ is a covariate but not listed in the methods.

## Tooling notes for the editor

- `check_anchor_numbers.py` cannot test the Voce and Boden anchors. Their PDFs are online-first versions numbered 1–11 and 1–8, as METHODS prescribes for anchors, but the script offsets pages from the print first page in the reference record (549, 987).
