## 1. The lists

**Legal frame (each passage read twice)**
- **Republic Act No. 9165** (Comprehensive Dangerous Drugs Act of 2002), Sec. 3(j): dangerous drugs "Include those listed in the Schedules annexed to the 1961 Single Convention" (as amended by the 1972 Protocol) and to the 1971 Convention, "as enumerated in the attached annex". Sec. 3(h) does the same for precursors (1988 Convention Tables I and II).
- Sec. 3 also defines (v) cannabis, (w) MDMA, (x) methamphetamine hydrochloride ("Shabu"), (y) opium and (z) opium poppy. Each defined drug covers "any of its isomers or derivatives in any form" (MDMA, methamphetamine). Cannabis covers the plant "in any form whatsoever". Opium covers "morphine or any alkaloid of opium".
- **Section 93** gives the Dangerous Drugs Board (DDB) the power to reclassify, add to or remove drugs. This is as stated in Board Regulation No. 1, s. 2014 and in the preamble of Board Regulation No. 3, s. 2015; the Act's own Section 93 was not reached (section 2).
- **DDB Board Regulation No. 1, Series of 2014** (index: in force 13 March 2015):
  - Sec. 1(o): dangerous drugs also include those the Board classifies under Section 93 "for domestic control".
  - Sec. 2(1): "Refer to Annex A for the Philippine Schedules."
  - Sec. 2(2): Section 93 substances "shall likewise be categorized under the Philippine Schedule 5".
  - Sec. 3(1)(a)(i): when the UN notifies a scheduling decision, the DDB must automatically issue an implementing regulation if no objection comes within 180 days.
- **DDB Board Regulation No. 1, Series of 2020**, "Immediate Adoption of Changes in the Scope of Control of Substances" under the three UN drug conventions. Only the title could be read (scanned PDF).

**The current list**
- **DDB, "Updated Lists of Scheduled Controlled Substances", "as of 20 JULY 2026"** (23 pages; footer "DDB-LEGAL 20 July 2026 - MTF"). It is the only list linked from the DDB's Regulatory Control page, as "UPDATED LIST OF CONTROLLED SUBSTANCES".
- **Controlled precursors and essential chemicals** (as of 03 December 2024): Table I has 27 rows, Table II 8. No item is among them.
- **1961 Convention part** ("AS OF 09 JUNE 2025"):
  - "List of Drugs Included in Schedule 1": 138 entries;
  - Schedule II: 10 entries;
  - Schedule III (exempted preparations): 8 entries;
  - Schedule IV: 18 entries.
- **1971 Convention part** ("AS OF 06 DECEMBER 2025"):
  - "List of Substances in Schedule 1": 35 rows, unnumbered;
  - "Substances in Schedule II": 64 rows, unnumbered;
  - Schedule III: 9 entries;
  - Schedule IV: 69 entries.
- **PHILIPPINE SCHEDULE part** ("Pursuant to Section 93, Article XI ..."). It says "the following are classified as controlled substances under PHILIPPINE SCHEDULE". It has two parts:
  - "DDB Regulations" (10 items): CBD drug products (Schedule 4), GBL, plants, ephedrine/pseudoephedrine (two items), Salvia divinorum, methylephedrine, nalbuphine, ketamine, thionyl chloride;
  - "Board issuances" (7 items): the esketamine prescription rule, four Lianhua Qingwen removals or extensions, and two ordinary-prescription measures.

**Philippine Schedules (Annex A to BR 1, s. 2014; read three times).** Annex A prints the criteria of Philippine Schedules 1 to 5, for example:
- Schedule 1: "has no currently accepted medical use in treatment in the Philippines".
- Schedules 3 to 5: "has a currently accepted medical use ...".
- Schedule 5: "Dangerous Drugs in the Philippines only".

Annex A also lines each Philippine schedule up with Convention schedules, but its table text is flattened (section 7, point 4). So no Philippine schedule number is given for Convention-listed items.

**Generic clauses.** These decide or qualify the following rows:
- **1961 lists:** isomers, esters and ethers, and salts (Schedule 1); isomers and salts (II); salts (IV).
- **1971 lists:** salts and stereoisomers (Schedule 1); salts (II, III); none printed (IV).
- **RA 9165 Sec. 3:** isomers or derivatives (MDMA, methamphetamine); "in any form whatsoever" (cannabis).
- **Ketamine regulation:** "including its salts, isomers, and salts of isomers". This decides the esketamine row, with the Board issuance.
- **GBL entry:** "All Compounds, Mixtures, Or Preparations Containing Any Quantity" of GBL.
- **Salvia regulation:** "its resin, extract, tincture or other forms". This bears on salvinorin A.
- **Plants regulation (title only):** plants containing listed substances, "or are sources thereof".
- **No analogue clause of general reach** appears anywhere.

## 3. How I searched

- **Finding the law.** WebSearch for the RA 9165 text with its annex on .gov.ph sites (Official Gazette, Senate, Congress, PDEA, DDB, Supreme Court E-Library). The DDB copy was the only official full text that could be read, and it lacks the annex.
- **Finding the lists.**
  - The DDB Board Regulations index was read series by series (2026 back to 2002). It led to BR 1, s. 2014 and its Annex A ("Philippine Schedules of Dangerous Drugs").
  - Annex A turned out to be a criteria table, not a substance list.
  - A search then found the DDB's consolidated "Updated Lists of controlled substances". It exists as an April 2023 file and as the current 20 July 2026 file. The DDB Regulatory Control page links only the 2026 file, and that file was used.
- **Currency.**
  - The list is "as of 20 JULY 2026"; its UN parts are dated 09 June 2025 (1961) and 06 December 2025 (1971).
  - It lags the index. The three 2026 inclusions in the index are not printed in the list (two full readings plus a targeted check): BR 2, s. 2026 (N-desethyl etonitazene), BR 3 (N-pyrrolidino isotonitazene) and BR 4 (MDMB-FUBINACA), all uploaded in August 2026. The only 2026 regulation the list mentions is BR 5, s. 2026.
  - None of the 2026 inclusions concerns the 77 items. No title in the index, 2002 to 2026 No. 5, names any not-found item (second index reading).
  - Searches for a law amending RA 9165's definitions or annex found none.
- **Regulations behind Philippine-only entries.** Those that could be read (ketamine 2005, Salvia 2015, BR 1, s. 2014) were read in full. The rest are scanned.
- **Limits of the summarizer.**
  - WebFetch would not quote passages over 125 characters, so long titles and clauses were read in numbered pieces.
  - The tables' cell boundaries are lost in the text layer. The rows of the 1971 Schedule 1 and II lists are unnumbered, so positions were counted in each reading.

## 7. Unsettled: for the editor

1. **The annex of RA 9165 was not read.** No official copy with the annex could be read (section 2). The DDB's consolidated list of 20 July 2026 was used as the current official list.
2. **Ten scanned regulations.** Their texts could not be read, so their conditions, adoption dates and any named plants are unknown. Among them is BR 1, s. 2020 (immediate adoption of UN changes). An OCR or text copy would settle this.
3. **Not-found rows and the UN.** BR 1, s. 2014, Sec. 3(1)(a)(i) and BR 1, s. 2020 tie the Philippine list to UN scheduling decisions. If the site's UN layer shows a not-found item (for example a 2C-x, NEP, 2-MMC or 4-BMC) under the 1961 or 1971 Convention, decide whether it is a Philippine dangerous drug although the DDB list does not print it.
4. **Philippine Schedule numbers.** Annex A's flattened table appears to line up as follows (three readings):
   - Philippine Schedule 1 with 1961 Schedule IV and 1971 Schedule I;
   - Philippine Schedule 2 with the labels "Schedule I Schedule II Schedule II";
   - Philippine Schedule 3 with 1971 Schedule III;
   - Philippine Schedule 4 with 1961 Schedule III and 1971 Schedule IV.

   This line-up is not used in the statuses. Section 93 items are recorded as Philippine Schedule 5 by BR 1, s. 2014, Sec. 2(2), and CBD products as Schedule 4, as printed.
5. **MDMB-4en-PINACA.** The list prints it in 1971 Schedule II, but the title of 2025 Board Regulation No. 13 says "Schedule I of the 1971 UN Convention". It is recorded per the list.
6. **CBD.** Only drug products with no more than 0.1 percent THC are named (Philippine Schedule 4). Other CBD forms are not stated:
   - plant extracts fall under RA 9165 Sec. 3(v) and the 1961 cannabis entry ("EXTRACTS and TINCTURES");
   - synthetic CBD is not addressed.
7. **Esketamine.** It is named only under "Board issuances", a prescription rule, and its control rests on ketamine's isomer clause. It is recorded as listed; please confirm.
8. **Salvinorin A.** It is not named, and whether "its resin, extract, tincture or other forms" covers it is for you. The row is draft.
9. **Ketamine's prescription exception** (Sec. 2 of BR 3, s. 2005). Whether later regulations changed it was not checked: BR 1, s. 2014, and the 2025-2026 ordinary-prescription measures.
10. **`medical_use` for Salvia divinorum and GBL** is null. Their Schedule 5 criteria in Annex A begin "has a currently accepted medical use", and whether to apply that is your call.
11. **"If named" plants.** The plants regulation (BR 3, s. 2019; title only) could reach DMT- or mescaline-containing plants. Its text could not be read to see whether it names any.
12. **Section 93 of RA 9165** was not read in an official copy of the Act. Its effect was taken from BR 1, s. 2014 and BR 3, s. 2015.
13. **The list lags the regulations.** The 20 July 2026 list does not print the three substances added by BR 2-4, s. 2026, so a later regulation can precede its listing. No index title through 2026 No. 5 names a not-found item, but the editor may want to re-check the index before publishing.
