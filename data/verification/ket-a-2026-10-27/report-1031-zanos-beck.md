- **cl_ket_acute_psychosis_dissociation** · beck_2020 · partly-supported · overstatement: As far as this source is concerned, two parts of the claim are not supported by it and one needs narrowing, though none is contradicted. (1) 'the changes pass within minutes to a couple of hours of the end of the infusion': Beck et al. describe the symptoms as transient throughout but give no recovery time and state in the Strengths and Limitations that further work is needed to characterise the time course of ketamine's psychotomimetic effects. The claim attributes its timing to Krystal et al. (1994) and Malhotra et al. (1996), which is the right attribution; nothing in the pooled result should be read as supporting it, and the sentence that opens the claim ('the changes pass within minutes to a couple of hours') sits before the Beck sentence, so a reader could take the meta-analysis to be the source. (2) 'with salt water given to the same people on another day as the comparison': in Beck's set the placebo was saline in 35 of the 36 healthy-volunteer studies (one, van Berckel et al. 1998, is 'not reported'), but the conditions were not always on another day. Two of the studies gave ketamine and placebo on the same day, and that design produced a significantly larger total-symptom effect (2.29, 95% CI 1.69-2.89, against 1.39, 1.12-1.66; P = .007), which Beck et al. suggest may reflect unblinding, since one of the two was unblinded and the other single-blind. (3) 'the psychosis-like effect being the larger of the two' is right as a pooled comparison, but Beck et al. note that the two analyses did not rest on the same studies (the negative analysis had an extra continuous-infusion study, the positive analysis more unblinded studies and fewer same-day studies), and they still call the negative effect large. Within those limits the wording does not overstate: the claim keeps the result as an association in screened healthy volunteers during one session, says the figures are peak values, reports the heterogeneity and the small-study asymmetry, and does not repeat the review's recommendation about how ketamine should be given, which would have read as advice. — Every Beck number in the statement and magnitude checks out against the Results, and I re-derived the three sample sizes from the Table: the 25 total-symptom studies sum to exactly 491 participants, the 21 positive-symptom studies to 513 and the 22 negative-symptom studies to 527, and all 36 rows sum to 725.
