**21 `cl_gen_drug_test_times`: very low, replicated, describes.** Verstraete (2004) and Moeller et al. (2017) give the
usual figures second-hand: one study per table cell, some of one or two people, mostly single low lab doses read at
detection limits (some rows at cutoffs); Moeller's Table 2 comes from textbooks, two laboratory FAQ pages, a newsletter
and older reviews, with no conditions. Goodwin et al. (2008), the one primary study, counts from admission, and 13 of
20 in the highest group were still positive when observation ended, so its means (4.6, 9.7, 15.4 days) are minimums.
New count: 43 of 46 people with both results had a positive after their first negative. Low start; risk of bias,
indirectness and imprecision −1 each. **Editor to decide:** status replicated (separate studies, reported with numbers
in the 2004 review, agree in direction) or single study (one primary study read); and adding page `heroin`, since how
long heroin shows is the most-viewed question and the claim carries the 63-men series and the 8-hour 6-acetylmorphine
window.

**22 `cl_gen_drug_test_false_positives`: very low, replicated, describes; draft (narrative reviews only).** Saitman
et al. (2014) and Moeller et al. (2017): each interference is specific to named tests, mostly case reports, chart
reviews or spiked urine. Frequencies: bupropion 41% (53 patients) of unconfirmed amphetamine screens, total unprinted;
sertraline 26.5% (26 samples) of false benzodiazepine screens, against Moeller's unexplained 27–32%; DMAA 92.3%;
efavirenz 46/50 against 47/50; ofloxacin 6/6. None is the chance that a person taking the medicine screens positive.
The reviews disagree on efavirenz and the Drug Screen Multi 5 (Moeller: affected, citing Blank 2009 and Röder 2007;
Saitman: negative in Blank 2009) and describe Rollins 1990 differently (2 false positives in 510 samples; "case
report").

**Not answerable from these papers:** screens that miss a drug (fentanyl, methadone, buprenorphine, oxycodone; lorazepam,
alprazolam, clonazepam; synthetic cannabinoids), which Moeller describes and people ask about, could be a separate
answer.

**Missing papers** (no DOIs printed): Blank et al. 2009 (Clin Infect Dis 48, 1787–1789) and Röder et al. 2007, to settle the

and Preston et al. 2002 (J Anal Toxicol 26, 393–400) for detection after heavy use.
