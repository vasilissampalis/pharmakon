# Final reports of the first readers (round pdf-2026-11-26), as each gave it to the editor

## Reader 745 (supplements; data/verification/pdf-2026-11-26/745_supplements.json, 45 items)
- Holze 2022 (Word file): Table S5 contradicts the paper's text: LSD 100 µg did not raise heart rate more than psilocybin (83 vs 78/82 bpm, not significant), while the card and claim say both LSD doses did. The table's peak values can replace the readings taken from Fig. 3. Placebo named correctly in 100% of sessions; right-drug guesses exactly 71/86/78/79%. Settles the anxiety, Tmax (a misprint for 0.75 h) and rate-pressure issues; checklist: 13–18 of 28 reported headache against 8 on placebo.
- Straumann 2023: nausea counts settle the contradiction (MDMA 14, LSD 7, both 11, of 24); placebo and MDMA alone recognised in 96–100% of sessions; effects up to 21 h with both drugs; the stated hypothesis unsupported on every outcome named primary.
- Smith 2022 (zip of TIFF figures): 93.6–97.3% of samples THC-dominant in each state; the S2 counts for balanced and CBD-dominant samples disagree with Fig 1 (2,192/991 vs 1,940/1,243).
- Sordo 2017: appendix 3 makes AMSTAR 2 item 9 partial-yes (rating stays critically low); Table B gives deaths after incomplete vs complete treatment (69 vs 17 and 84 vs 26 per 1,000 person-years), which the claim says the review lacks.
- Darke 2017: the appendix lists the 10 cases with no clinical detail; one rests on a prosecutor's letter.
- Timmermann 2021, Nayak 2022: the files saved as supplements are byte-identical copies of the articles; Hysek 2014's is the online-first version of the article. Supplements still missing: 10.1038/s41598-021-01209-2, 10.3389/fpsyg.2022.852248, 10.1093/scan/nst161.
- Reviewer comments sit inside card fields (Holze key findings 3 and 6, Smith 11 locator, Sordo 11). No reference record disagrees with its file.

## Reader 737 (cocaine and the nose)
- sc_nitro_2022 (AMSTAR 2 critically low): the abstract mislabels sites (Table 4 and Fig. 2: inferior turbinate and maxillary wall 59%, palate 29.9%; the abstract gives the side wall 29.9%); 12 of 17 series selected people by site; none followed anyone after stopping. Table 6 mixes biopsy and patient counts; series from the same groups may share patients.
- sc_saibene_2025 (narrative checklist: high): "multidisciplinary" in the abstract, but the panel was 18 rhinologists who are the authors, working mostly from reviews; "no safe threshold" and "only stopping halts lesions" are opinion, each resting on one review; repair waits for 12 months of abstinence proven by drug tests. Items 2.2 and 4.1 break its own outlier rule (totals may be 19 consensus and 4 near-consensus); Appendix A missing from the file; the introduction cites Nitro for quality-of-life data Nitro does not report.
- sc_trimarchi_2001 (JBI: high): no abstract; the summary says systemic inflammation "universally absent", yet CRP abnormal in 9 of 18; repeat imaging only for 4 people who kept using, all worse. Figure 3's percentages contradict the text and Table 3 (use counts out of 16 imaged); two p-values not reproducible (sex 0.037, Fisher gives 0.53; per-patient vasculitis 0.11, gives 0.74).
- Healing after stopping: none of the three has follow-up data on people who stopped.
- Missing companions: Trimarchi et al. 2003, Am J Rhinol 17:33–43 (progression data; DOI not in the files); Trimarchi et al. 2014, Rhinology, 10.4193/Rhino13.112 (sole support for "no safe threshold"); Nitro's PROSPERO protocol CRD42021240014.
- Reference record trimarchi_2001: "wegener" in lower case and author names in capitals.

## Reader 732 (Seven questions B)
- sc_open_science_collaboration_2015: replication power (0.92) computed against the original effects, which the paper calls inflated; teams picked feasible studies; 13 of 113 replications never finished; the authors say a failed replication does not prove the original false; the breakdown by P value adds to 34 significant replications, not 35. The 36% comes from one replication each, in three 2008 psychology journals.
- sc_ioannidis_2005: the "proof" is a formula on assumed inputs (interpretation gap substantial); Table 4 reproduces exactly; one Box 1 value (1.5×10⁻⁴) does not follow from the essay's formula (about 2.5×10⁻⁴). Qualifies "significant means probably not chance": a result with P < 0.05 can still be more likely false than true.
- sc_sumner_2014: "exaggeration" means stronger than the paper, not unjustified; each news story coded with its press release (not blind); the overall 47% for claims about humans conflicts with its two conditional rates (about 41%); the "no effect on coverage" intervals allow increases of 21 to 27 points. Its scale rates "linked to" as ambiguous.
- sc_boutron_2010: only 72 of 616 trials analysed; reader agreement κ 0.47 for abstract conclusions; a funding–spin link asserted without analysis; Table 3 prints 0.4 for 4.0.
- Missing companions: the comment and reply Science lists with OSC (351(6277):1037; the reader's DOIs for them were from memory, unverified); supplements of OSC, Sumner (SI11–12) and Boutron not in the files. All four reference records agree with the PDFs.

## Reader 742 (tramadol)
- sc_rostam_abadi_2020 (AMSTAR 2 critically low): the abstract's 34.6% seizures and 0.7% deaths have prediction intervals 12.5–60.9% and 0–10%; the 5.7% share of death records pools four datasets, led by unpublished data; "as prevalent as illicit opioids" has no figures behind it; no included study measured individual risk. One hospital is 5.9% in Table 4 but 0.8% in Figure 4; several pooled series probably share patients (Kermanshah, Tehran, Hamedan); the Discussion's "sole cause" for 336 deaths is not supported by the tables. Data S1–S5 not on file (S4 would settle AMSTAR item 7). Online-first PDF ("PDF p. N").
- sc_ryan_2015 (JBI moderate): 51 of 71 patients took other medicines too; coma and respiratory depression never split out for tramadol alone; no serotonin toxicity in 0 of 71 only rules out rates above 5.1%, yet the conclusion extends it to therapeutic doses; benzodiazepine count 16 in Table 1 vs 17 in the text. Online-first PDF.
- sc_senay_2003 (JBI high): "nearly 40%" is 33% in Table 3; rates are reported cases only (authors cite about 10% capture), stated three ways; manufacturer-funded; age denominator 455 matches no case count. Record writes "ultram®" (PDF "Ultram®"). Companion: Cicero et al., 1999, Drug Alcohol Depend 57, 7–22 (DOI not looked up).

## Reader 733 (addiction, the brain and recovery)
- sc_hart_2017: a one-page opinion piece with no reference list; "vast majority never become addicted" is never given a number; every figure in it has no source.
- sc_hart_2020: the 70–90% who never meet use-disorder criteria is cited only to his own book; "no neurobiological substrate" is cited to a review about methamphetamine and thinking skills; "effects overwhelmingly positive" rests on one lab study.
- sc_heyman_2013: "remission" means not meeting dependence criteria in the past year, one drug at a time, not quitting; the survey curves cover cocaine, cannabis, alcohol and nicotine, not heroin; by his own account nobody had tested whether treatment changes remission in population data. He misquotes Robins: the 6% "treated" were men with a positive urine test at departure, and 12% is the 3-year re-addiction rate for all men addicted in Vietnam, so the often-repeated "70% relapse with treatment, under 12% without" mixes groups and time spans. Text 0.11 for cigarettes against 0.015 and 0.024 in Figure 3; Figure 7 looks like the age spread of current cases, not remission by age.
- sc_robins_1993: 45% tried narcotics in Vietnam and 20% reported addiction; 5% addicted again in the first year back, 12% over 3 years; Robins says Vietnam's conditions were unprecedented. "6% overall re-addicted" in the text, but her Figure 6 shows about 12%; no total sample size in the text.
- Companions: Lopez-Quintero et al. (2011), Addiction 106:657–69, 10.1111/j.1360-0443.2010.03194.x (source of Heyman's curves); Robins, Davis & Nurco (1974), American Journal of Public Health 64 supplement (no DOI recorded). All four reference records agree with the PDFs.
