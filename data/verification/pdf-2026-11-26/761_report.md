# Reader 761: tramadol (items 17–18), 26 November 2026

Ryan & Isbister (2015) and Senay et al. (2003) read in full; Rostam-Abadi et al. (2020) pp. 1–3, 7, 13–25 and 27 (prevalence pages not needed). Every table and figure used was checked on a rendered page (forest plots at 300 dpi). Arithmetic: exact binomial intervals, pool totals, Senay's shares (all reproduce).

## Claims

**17. `cl_opi_tramadol_overdose`** (new, section 6). Rests on 71 Australian admissions after more than 400 mg (51 with other medicines) and the Iranian review's pools. Seizures 8/71 (11 in 100; 5–21), as often after tramadol alone (2/20) as with other medicines (6/51); coma and respiratory depression (13/71) are not split for tramadol alone. Iran: seizures 34.6 in 100 (prediction interval 12.5–60.9), deaths 0.7 in 100 (0–10.0). Serotonin toxicity 0/71 rules out only rates above 5.1 in 100 in such overdoses. **Very low** (low −1 risk of bias, −1 inconsistency); status replicated. Naloxone response and seizure timing are stated as hospital findings only, never as signs or steps.

**18. `cl_opi_tramadol_withdrawal`** (new, section 8). Senay: 422 of 1,248 reports rated withdrawal (33 in 100 in Table 3, not the abstract's nearly 40), 55 atypical (1 in 8); reported cases only, capture about 10 in 100 by the FDA estimate the authors cite (later under 5, their own estimate); manufacturer-funded; no comparison with other opioids. Plus two Iranian case reports from the review. **Very low** (low −2 risk of bias); single study. The paper's management advice (restarting, benzodiazepines) is left out: about 75 consultations, no counts.

## Missing papers that would change these

- Seizures at prescribed doses (not measured here): Gasse et al. (2000), Pharmacotherapy 20, 629–634; Gardner et al. (2000), Pharmacotherapy 20, 1423–1431 (both in Ryan's references; no DOI printed).
- Serotonin toxicity with antidepressants: Nelson & Philbrick (2012), Ann Pharmacother 46, 1712–1716.
- Whether atypical withdrawal is particular to tramadol: Fishbain et al. (1988), J Clin Psychopharmacol 8, 441–442, a letter in Senay's references titled as an atypical withdrawal syndrome after oxycodone detoxification. Also Cicero et al. (1999), Drug Alcohol Depend 57, 7–22 (the exposure estimate behind the rates).
- Rostam-Abadi supplements Data S1–S5.

## Cards and records against the PDFs

No number on the three cards disagrees with the PDFs. Points the cards miss or state too strongly:
- `sc_ryan_2015`: its timeline calls Spiller and Marquardt US series; Ryan does not say so.
- `sc_rostam_abadi_2020`: (a) the adverse-reaction data give 249 people, but Table 5 splits them 117 men and 122 women (239), and gives seizures as 29.5% against 29.3% in the text. (b) Table 5 lists bradypnoea 18.0% for one series (Eizadi-Mood 2011), which the card leaves out. (c) By the editors' approximation from Figure 6's rounded proportions, about 102 of the about 129 deaths in the death pool come from the single 10.0% series.
- `senay_2003` record: "ultram®" should be "Ultram®" (a brand name), as the first reader noted. The other two records match.
