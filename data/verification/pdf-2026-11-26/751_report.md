## Verdicts

- **linked: partly.** "Go together without causing" and "people who use a drug may differ in other ways" rest on Hill, Cohen
  (Berkson's bias), Sumner and Robins (heroin's extra problems vanished once behaviour before service and other drug use were
  held constant). "We say causes only when studies can show it" is site policy, qualified by Hill (no viewpoint decisive or
  required). "Most of what we know ... is linked to" rests on no paper; proposed replacement from Sumner (a third of press
  releases made correlational findings sound more causal).
- **q1: supported** for treatment and hospital (Cohen; Robins: 5 and 12 in 100 re-addicted against two-thirds of treated
  civilians; "treated or arrested" samples). No paper names prison.
- **q2: supported** (Hill's temporality; Robins 1975, p. 959; Robins 1993, p. 1046).
- **q3: partly.** Amount and frequency matter (Hill; Robins 1993); "one dose in a lab is not daily use" has no source here.
- **q4: needs rewording.** "Significant means probably not chance" is what ASA principle 2 rules out (also Gigerenzer,
  Ioannidis). The proposal adds the converse error (Hill; principle 6; Boutron, 10 of 72 abstracts). The normal-range
  sentence has no source here.
- **q5, q6, q7: supported** (Gigerenzer; Open Science Collaboration, 35 of 97; Sumner, Boutron), with qualifications in the
  file (Hill: ratios matter for judging causes, one unrepeatable study can convince; OSC: a failed repetition is not proof of
  falsehood).
