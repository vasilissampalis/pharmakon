## Verdicts

- **linked: partly.** "Things can go together without one causing the other" and "people who use a drug may differ in other
  ways" rest on Hill, Cohen (Berkson's bias), Sumner and Robins 1975 and 1993 (heroin's extra problems vanished once
  behaviour before service and other drug use were held constant). "We say causes only when the studies can show it" is site
  policy; Hill qualifies it (no viewpoint is decisive or required). "Most of what we know ... is linked to" rests on no paper;
  proposed replacement from Sumner (a third of press releases made correlational findings sound more causal).
- **q1: supported** for treatment and hospital (Cohen's mechanism; Robins: 5 and 12 in 100 re-addicted against two-thirds of
  treated civilians; earlier studies built on "treated or arrested" people). No paper names prison.
- **q2: supported** (Hill's temporality; Robins 1975, p. 959; Robins 1993, p. 1046).
- **q3: partly.** Amount and frequency matter (Hill's gradient; Robins 1993); "one dose in a lab is not daily use" has no
  source here.
- **q4: needs rewording.** "Significant means probably not chance" is what ASA principle 2 rules out; Gigerenzer list it
  among textbook errors; in Ioannidis's model a P < 0.05 result can be more likely false than true. The proposal adds the
  converse error (Hill; principle 6; Boutron, 10 of 72 abstracts). The normal-range sentence has no source here.
- **q5, q6, q7: supported** (Gigerenzer; Open Science Collaboration, 35 of 97; Sumner, Boutron), with qualifications: ratios
  matter for judging causes and absolute numbers need a comparison; a failed repetition is not proof of falsehood; one
  unrepeatable observation can sometimes convince.
