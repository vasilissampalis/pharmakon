## Verdicts

- **linked: partly.** "Things can go together without one causing the other" and "people who use a drug may differ in other
  ways" rest on Hill, Cohen (Berkson's bias), Sumner and Robins 1975 and 1993 (heroin's extra problems vanished once behaviour
  before service and other drug use were held constant). "We say causes only when the studies can show it" is site policy;
  Hill qualifies it (no viewpoint is decisive or required; observational evidence can sometimes support a cause). "Most of
  what we know about drugs in people is linked to" rests on no paper: proposed replacement from Sumner (a third of press
  releases made correlational findings sound more causal).
- **q1: supported** for treatment and hospital (Cohen's mechanism; Robins: 5 and 12 in 100 re-addicted against two-thirds of
  treated civilians; earlier studies built on "treated or arrested" people, Robins 1975). No paper names prison.
- **q2: supported** (Hill's temporality; Robins 1975, p. 959; Robins 1993, p. 1046).
- **q3: partly.** That amount and frequency matter: Hill's gradient, Robins 1993. "One dose in a lab is not daily use": no
  source here.
- **q4: needs rewording.** "Significant means probably not chance" is what the ASA statement rules out (principle 2);
  Gigerenzer list the same reading among textbook errors; in Ioannidis's model results with P < 0.05 are more likely false than
  true at low power and prior odds. The proposed text adds the converse error (Hill; principle 6; Boutron: 10 of 72 abstracts).
  The normal-range sentence has no source here.
- **q5, q6, q7: supported** (Gigerenzer; Open Science Collaboration, 35 of 97 repeated; Sumner, Boutron), with the
  qualifications in the file: ratios matter for judging causes (Hill); absolute numbers need a comparison (Gigerenzer); a
  failed repetition does not prove a result false (OSC); one unrepeatable observation can sometimes convince (Hill).

## Sources that would fill the gaps

  2026: 176 of 412 claims worded as associations, 36 causal, none "causes"), which support "few of our answers say causes",
  not "most is linked to".
- q1, prison: a study comparing people who use drugs in prison with community samples.
- q2, stronger: a prospective cohort measured before first use (Robins's "before" data were mostly asked after return).
- q3: studies giving a drug once and repeatedly (tolerance), or a methods review of laboratory doses and volunteers against
  use outside the laboratory; on the site, each drug's checked tolerance answer.
- q4, normal range: a study giving the share of individuals within norms beside the group difference. The nearest checked
  claim, `cl_meth_cognition_normal_range`, says a group average in the normal range does not mean every person is (a quarter
  to a third met an impairment definition in two studies), so "almost everyone" would overstate there.
