# Reader 756: what snorting cocaine does to the nose (item 9), 26 November 2026

All three PDFs read in full; every number used checked on a rendered page.

## Claim

**`cl_coc_nose_damage`** (new, section 9, 28 anchors, schema passes). Nitro 2022: 127 people with lesions in 17 retrospective series (Table 4, Fig. 2). Trimarchi 2001: 18 people against 21 with Wegener granulomatosis; imaging counts out of 16; repeat imaging only in 4 who kept using, all worse. Saibene 2025: opinion of 18 rhinologists (repair only after 12 months of drug-test-proven abstinence, a period from the DSM-5 remission definition, not healing data). **Very low**: descriptive, starts low; risk of bias −1 (12 of 17 series chosen by site or presentation, possible shared patients, mixed methods), imprecision −1. Status replicated: septal perforation in nearly everyone in all 17 series; the shares come from one review. Stated plainly: nobody followed people after stopping or counted how many who snort get this.

## PDFs against cards and records

- Nitro: the abstract gives 59% and 29.9% to the nasal floor and inferolateral wall; Table 4 and Fig. 2 to the inferior turbinate/medial maxillary wall and the palate (card right). Its Table 3 counts for Trimarchi 2001 mix clinical, imaging and follow-up findings (inferior turbinate 18 from a clinical sentence; floor 1 against 4 imaged; 2 orbital infections). Card key finding 7 omits the Discussion's view that Trimarchi 2003 supports the spread hypothesis.
- Trimarchi: Fig. 3's percentages fit 11, 6, 3, 2 and 5 of 18, not the counts of 16. Sex p 0.037 (Fisher 0.53) and per-person vasculitis p 0.11 (Fisher 0.74) do not reproduce. "Universally absent" systemic inflammation conflicts with abnormal CRP in 9 and ESR in 8 of 18. "57% of patients" with vascular changes is 25 of 44 biopsies.
- Saibene: numbers agree with the card; the card calls ref. 31 a narrative review, which the PDF does not say.
- Records: `trimarchi_2001` authors in capitals, "wegener" lower case; `saibene_2025` "young-IfOS" against the PDF's "Young-IfOS".
