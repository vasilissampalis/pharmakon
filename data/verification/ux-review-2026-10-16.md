# Usability review of the prototype (independent AI reviewer, 2026-10-16)

An AI reviewer who had not seen how the site was built judged the published page as a visitor (phone 390×844 and desktop
1280×900, Playwright with Chromium; about 70 search queries; axe-core 4.10.2). Usability only: no fix adds dosing,
administration, combination or sourcing content. Screenshots and scripts: `scratchpad/ux-review/` (session scratchpad).
The editor's decisions on each finding are in the table at the end.

## Top five
1. Emergency help is reachable only from the top of a page: the header scrolls away; the methamphetamine page (about 151
   phone screens) has no emergency link in its content, not even in "Acute risks"; search puts the Emergency page 12th of
   12 for "overdose", 4th for "emergency" and "help", and not at all for "poison" or "naloxone"; "Numbers" in the menu
   means statistics.
2. Search misses or misranks street names: K, G, H, E and X return nothing and say nothing; "ice" ranks methamphetamine
   9th (it matches inside "practice"); "acid" lists GHB first and LSD 4th; "weed" lists PCP first; "ecstasy" lists GHB
   first; "oxy" never shows prescription opioids; misspellings ("metamphetamine", "extasy") fail.
3. The methamphetamine page has no bottom line on its first screen (metadata first), and its safety sections start about
   56 phone screens down; phones have no section navigation once scrolled.
4. Section links can open the wrong drug: section addresses (#sec-13) are shared across pages, so a shared "Legal
   status" link from Ketamine opens methamphetamine's legal status; mistyped page addresses silently show methamphetamine.
5. Internal workflow words are shown to readers ("PLANNED", "METADATA VERIFIED", "AI ANCHORED", "re-found blind",
   "METHODS §1.5", the POPULATION/EXPOSURE/COMPARATOR blocks, mechanism codes as card descriptions); a green
   "METADATA VERIFIED" next to a claim reads as "this claim is verified".

## Other findings (6–24)
6. Planned pages open with metadata; the "On this page now" line is on the second phone screen; 11–12 of 16 sections are
   empty.
7. Every claim opens in full technical detail (plain text is about a quarter of a claim's height on a phone).
8. "Not medical advice" and the draft status appear only at the bottom; "Conflicts of interest: … to be declared".
9. Phone search results hide their type labels; open questions lead to pages with no answer; landing on a claim shows no
   drug name.
10. Phone navigation hides five of nine items with no cue; "Numbers" is ambiguous.
11. Some information exists only on mouse hover (statistics source line, interaction cells; "hover" in instructions).
12. Interactions is a top-level dead end (an empty grid).
13. Reserved and unread sections speak in internal terms (section 7, section 11).
14. Much supporting text is under 12px (29.7% of characters on Substances).
15. Many stand-alone controls are too small to tap (breadcrumbs 16px, "Why this rating" 17px, footer 112/166 links).
16. Screen-reader and keyboard orientation: one page title for every route; no lang attribute; no skip link; Emergency
    shown first on phones but fifth in reading order; search results without list semantics; heading jump on Substances.
17. Section names are written for specialists; inside Identity, street names come after chemical identifiers.
18. The lower part of the home page is a project dashboard.
19. The substance index leads with mechanism codes and paper counts.
20. "How to read a claim" promises two minutes but puts the key last.
21. Statistics page: 39 filter buttons before the first figure; unclear captions ("Last year use"; unlabelled ranges).
22. The error form requires a DOI or PMID even for "Language or clarity" reports.
23. The glossary (51 entries) has no A–Z index; entries open with the technical definition.
24. The editors' review queue is visible to anyone with the address; a sort menu makes that page scroll sideways.

## What already works
The Emergency page itself; Emergency first in the phone menu, in red; plain sentences before technical ones; the
question-and-short-answer pattern of "Largest uncertainties"; tap popovers for citations and terms; certainty dots with
"Why this rating"; honesty about the project's status; desktop's sticky contents list; text that scales with the
phone's setting; a visible focus ring.

## Appendix: search tests (phone)
Failing or misranked: ice (meth 9 of 12), metamphetamine / methamfetamine / μεθαμφεταμίνη (none), K / E / X / G / H
(none, no message), ecstasy (MDMA 2nd), extasy (MDMA absent), weed (cannabis 2nd), crack (cocaine 2nd), mephedrone
(cathinones 2nd), acid (LSD 4 of 9), oxy (prescription opioids absent), lean (2nd), speed (2nd), poppers (2nd), 2C-B
(2nd), overdose (Emergency 12 of 12), emergency / help (4 of 4), poison / naloxone (absent). Working: meth, tina,
crystal meth, ket, special k, molly, mandy, xanax, benzos, spice, K2, coke, charlie, smack, shrooms, GHB, heroin,
fentanyl, nitrous, bath salts, ambulance, 112.

## Editor's decisions (2026-10-16, site published as artifact versions 29 and 30)

| # | Finding | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Emergency help only at the top | Done: phones show a fixed "Call 112 · Emergency" bar (hidden on the Emergency page itself); every substance page opens with "If this is happening now: 112 · 166 · Emergency page", repeated at the top of sections 6 and 7; the Emergency page is the first search result for overdose, emergency, help, poison, naloxone, ambulance, 112, 166 and Greek equivalents, with the numbers in the result; the home card is first and shows 112; "Numbers" renamed "Statistics"; Emergency first in the menu's markup. Numbers for the other countries the site covers added in version 31: 911 (United States, Canada), 999 (United Kingdom), 000 (Australia), each from two official sources. |
| 2 | Search misses street names | Done: exact names first (the page's name and the editors' list before names found only in sourced records), then the start of a name, then a one- or two-letter slip ("closest name"); one-letter names (K, G, H, E, X) work; whole-word matching only; Greek names added for search (`data/site/aliases-el.json`, not shown as facts); "No match" says so and offers the drug list; result types shown on phones; arrow keys move through the results; a live count for screen readers. Every query in the review's appendix was rerun on the new build, with a few more (unconscious, not breathing, seizure): the expected drug or page now comes first for each. |
| 3 | No bottom line on the methamphetamine page | Done in part: the page opens with a one-line draft notice and the emergency line; the project metadata moved into "About this page"; "Best established" and "Largest uncertainties" come first and are no longer underlined paragraphs ("See the evidence →"); every section starts closed on phones (page height 127,748 → 6,670 px) and the section index stays at the top while scrolling. An "In short" box with shortened claims was not added: shortening the plain sentences would drop their caveats; this is left to the senior author. |
| 4 | Links open the wrong drug | Done: section links carry the drug (#s-ketamine/sec-13); unknown addresses get a "Page not found" page with suggestions; old #sec-N links open that section of the page last shown. |
| 5 | Workflow words shown to readers | Done in part: claim stamps read "Draft: not yet checked by our editor" (the ladder step is in the label's tooltip); status labels in plain words and one neutral colour (found by several teams, repeated by one team only, one study, results point both ways, experts disagree); substance cards and search show "Draft" or "Basics only"; study-detail field names in plain words. The Methods page keeps the protocol's own terms. |
| 6 | Planned pages | Done: "Basics only so far" notice with large buttons (names and street names, official figures, legal status, open questions); the empty sections are listed once under "Not written yet" (Ketamine page 4,573 → 1,763 px on a phone). |
| 7 | Claims open in full detail | Done: plain sentence and certainty shown; the precise statement, study details, evidence anchors and tools sit under "Precise statement, study details and sources (n)", open by default on wide screens; a page-wide switch shows or hides them all. |
| 8 | Not-medical-advice line only at the bottom | Done: in the notice under each title. The conflict-of-interest declaration stays "to be declared" until the senior author makes it (a decision in the Review tab). |
| 9 | Search results on phones | Done (types shown; open questions link to section 14 and say "Not answered yet"). |
| 10 | Phone menu | Done in part: Emergency first in the markup; "Statistics". A "More" menu was not added. |
| 11 | Hover-only information | Done: the statistics source line opens on a tap; instructions say "tap (or hover)". Interaction cells: their questions are listed below the grid, and the page says so. |
| 12 | Interactions dead end | Done: "not written yet" in the eyebrow, and a first line with 112, 166 and the Poison Centre. |
| 13 | Reserved sections | Done: sections 7 and 11 reworded in plain words; internal reading-list numbers removed from the section notes (monograph v0.4.2, no claim changed). |
| 14–15 | Small text, small tap targets | Done in part: phone minimums for badges, card captions and field labels; stand-alone controls (breadcrumbs, "Why this rating", "How this row was read", claim tools, footer links, details toggles) at least 44 px tall on phones. |
| 16 | Orientation for screen readers | Done: a title per page; lang="en" (and lang="el" on Greek text in the template); focus moves to the heading after a page change; "Skip to content"; Emergency first in the reading order; heading order fixed on the drug index. axe: no violations on 9 routes in light and dark mode. |
| 17 | Section names; order inside Identity | Order done (version 30): "This page covers", street names, forms and drug checking first; the chemical names and identifiers fold away; the method note sits under "How these facts were checked". Section titles not changed: they are part of the protocol's template (and the title of section 12 is an open decision); left to the senior author. |
| 18 | Home page dashboard | Done (version 30): "How a sentence gets onto this site" and "Where the project stands" fold away under their headings (phone page 7,271 → 4,828 px). |
| 19 | Substance index | Done in part: "Find a drug"; street names larger, before the mechanism line; "papers on the reading list" instead of counts with "priority". |
| 20 | How to read | Done (version 30): the list of a claim's parts comes first, then the live example, fully open; label descriptions updated. |
| 21 | Statistics filters | Done in part (version 30): one menu replaces the row of 39 buttons (first figure now at 865 px on a phone, was 1,418). Version 33: ranges say what they are ("Range across countries", from the data's own interval type) and prevalence captions say the period ("use in the last 12 months", "ever used", "last 30 days", as the glossary defines them); the sources' captions are kept. |
| 22 | Error form | Done: a source is needed only for factual reports; "About" names the page and section in words. |
| 23 | Glossary index | Done (version 30): a filter field above the 51 terms. Entries keep their order and wording. |
| 24 | Review queue | Done: the sideways scroll fixed. The queue is visible only to the owner's signed-in view of a private page; the label stays. |
