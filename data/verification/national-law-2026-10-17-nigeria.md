## 2. How Nigerian law lists these drugs

### NDLEA Act (government copy)

The Act has no substance list of its own. Instead, s. 52 defines its terms by reference to the UN schedules.

- **"Narcotic Drug":** substances "in the First schedule of the single convention of Narcotic Drug 1961".
  - It adds "as amended by the 1972 protocol" and "as amended in the second schedule to this Act".
  - It also brings in the 1988 Convention, which is printed as "1989".
- **"Psychotropic Substances":** substances "specified in Schedules I, II, III and IV of the convention" of 1971.
- **No date for the reference.** Neither definition gives a date, and neither says "as amended from time to time".
- **Plants:** s. 52 also defines three plants.
  - "Cannabis plant": the genus is printed "Cannabii".
  - "Coca bush": the genus is printed "Erythrolos".
  - "Opium poppy": Papaver somniferum.
- **Second Schedule:** this lists precursor chemicals only, with a salts clause under each table. None of them is one of our items.
  - Table I: Ephedrine; Ergometrine; Ergotamine; "Hysergic Acid"; Phenylacetic acid; "1-pheny 1-2 propone"; pseudoephedrine.
  - Table II: "Accetic Anbydride"; Acetone; "Anitranlic Acid"; Ethyl ether; Piperidine.
- **Other drugs named in the Act:**
  - The offence provisions ss. 11, 12, 13, 16 and 19 name "cocaine, LSD, heroine". These words are recorded only as names; no penalty is recorded.
  - s. 20 names opium, poppy, coca bush and cannabis plant, in connection with cultivation.
  - The term "Indian hemp" appears nowhere in the Act, and neither does "heroin" spelled without the final e.

### Dangerous Drugs Act (PLAC copy)

- **Part I (s. 4):** raw opium, coca leaves and Indian hemp.
- **Part III (s. 8(1)(a) to (i)):**
  - (a) medical opium.
  - (b) extracts and tinctures of Indian hemp.
  - (c) morphine, diacetylmorphine ("diamorphine or heroin") and the other esters of morphine.
  - (d) cocaine (including synthetic cocaine), ecgonine and the esters of ecgonine.
  - (e) solutions and preparations above threshold strengths.
  - (f) any preparation containing diacetylmorphine.
  - (g) dihydrohydroxycodeinone, dihydrocodeinone, dihydromorphinone, acetyldihydrocodeinone and dihydromorphine; morphine-N-oxide and other pentavalent nitrogen morphine derivatives.
  - (h) thebaine, benzylmorphine and the other ethers of morphine. Codeine and dionin are excepted here.
  - (i) preparations of the substances in (g) and (h).
- **Powers to add drugs:**
  - s. 8(3) lets the President apply Part III by order to new derivatives or salts of morphine or cocaine, other alkaloids of opium, or "any other drug of whatever kind". The condition is ill effects "analogous to those produced by morphine or cocaine".
  - s. 9(4) lets the President apply Part III by order to codeine and dionin.
  - s. 9(1) bars trade in new products of opium phenanthrene alkaloids or coca ecgonine alkaloids that were not in medical use on 13 July 1931.
- **Seven application orders (1928 to 1955) add 24 names.** Among them:
  - amidone (41 of 1950, item (b));
  - methylmorphine "(commonly known as codeine)" and ethylmorphine "(commonly known as dionin)" (L.N. 83 of 1955);
  - pethidine and dihydrocodeine (L.N. 85 of 1955).
- **Relaxations and exemptions:**
  - The relaxation orders take some preparations out of Part III: opium, morphine, cocaine, diamorphine, Dicodide and Eucodal preparations.
  - The Dangerous Drugs Regulations exempt some opium, morphine and diamorphine preparations.
  - The Modified Form Regulations (L.N. 88 of 1955) apply modified rules to codeine, dionin, morpholinylethylmorphine and dihydrocodeine.

### Indian Hemp Act (PLAC copy; FAOLEX copy agrees)

- s. 1 defines "Indian hemp" as any plant or part of a plant of the genus cannabis, the separated resin and any preparation containing that resin.
- The Act names no other drug.
- It makes an exception for "any medical preparation of Indian hemp" handled without an offence under the Dangerous Drugs Act. This is recorded as `medical_use`, the only one in the file.

### NAFDAC FAQ (government)

The FAQ gives import-permit categories, each with examples:

- **Narcotic Drugs (5):** Fentanyl, Methadone, Pethidine, Morphine, oxycodone.
- **Psychotropic Drugs (12):** Bromazepam, Alprazolam, Diazepam, Methylphenidate, Flunitrazepam, Pentazocine, "Phenobaribital", Buprenorphine, Lorazepam, Midazolam, Zolpidem, Clonazepam.
- **Nationally Controlled Drugs (9):** Caffeine, "Chlroamphenicol", Dextromethorphan, Phenylephrine HCl, Ketamine, Thiopental, Imipramine, "Amitryptyline", Tramadol HCl.
- **Precursor Chemicals (9).**

These are examples only, and the page does not state the legal basis for any category.

## 4. Method

**Readings:**
- Every chunk was read twice, with differently worded prompts and output formats, and the two readings were compared by an inline Python script. Where they disagreed, the chunk was read a third time.
- The chunks were:
  - NDLEA Act: s. 52 (plus an earlier descriptive reading), the Second Schedule (3 readings), the names outside the Second Schedule, and the version line.
  - Dangerous Drugs Act: ss. 1-10, the application orders, the list of subsidiary instruments, the decisive wording (s. 8(3) read a third time), and the preparation lists at substance level.
  - Indian Hemp Act: 2 readings on PLAC and 1 on FAOLEX.
  - NAFDAC FAQ: 3 readings.
  - Yellow List: names (2) and version (2).
  - NDLEA post of 2025: 2 readings.
- WebFetch will not quote much more than about 125 characters. So long definitions were read as short decisive phrases, and lists were read as names.

**Matching:**
- A script matched the 77 items to the names found in the readings. It used only synonyms printed in the texts themselves ("commonly known as codeine"; "diamorphine or heroin") or in the Yellow List:

  | Yellow List name | Item |

  | dihydrocodeinone | hydrocodone |
  | Methylmorphine | codeine |
  | Diacetylmorphine | heroin |
  | Eucodal | oxycodone |
  | Dicodid(e) | hydrocodone |

- UN placements came from the site's UN layer.
- I checked every match by eye. One script result was overridden: it had applied each page's single UN status to the two "if named" items. Neither item is named in any list read, so neither gets an entry.

## 6. Disagreements between readings, and how each was settled

1. **NDLEA Second Schedule, last line of Table I.**
   - Reading 1 gave two entries, "1-pheny 1-2 propone" and "pseudoephedrine:". Reading 2 gave one run-on name.
   - A third reading found the line run on in the extracted text, most likely two entries given the column layout.
   - Recorded as printed. No item is affected.
2. **NDLEA Second Schedule, notes under the tables.**
   - Reading 1 paraphrased "whenever such salts are possible". Readings 2 and 3 agree on "whenever the existence of such salts is possible".
   - Settled by readings 2 and 3.
3. **NDLEA s. 52, "Cannabis plant".**
   - Reading 2 listed the defined term. Reading 1 listed only the genus in its name column, though the term was in its quoted phrase.
   - Not a real disagreement.
4. **NDLEA s. 52, the 1988 Convention clause.**
   - Reading 1 had no question on this clause. Reading 2 quoted it, and the earlier descriptive reading agrees with reading 2.
5. **Dangerous Drugs Act, ss. 1-10.**
   - The drug names agree in every provision. The spelling of misprints differs: reading 2 kept "ecgonme", "diacety lmorphine" and "Papaver somniforum", while reading 1 normalised them.
   - No item turns on these spellings. The misprints are not quoted in the JSON.
6. **Dangerous Drugs Act, 1950 order.** Reading 1 gave "beta pro dine" and reading 2 "betaprodine", a spacing difference only. Not one of our items.
7. **Dangerous Drugs Act, s. 8(3).**
   - Reading 1 quoted only the adding power. Reading 2 also listed new derivatives and salts of morphine and cocaine, other opium alkaloids, and "any other drug of whatever kind".
   - A third reading confirmed reading 2 and gave the condition: ill effects analogous to morphine or cocaine.
8. **Dangerous Drugs Act, s. 10 proviso.** The two readings quoted overlapping parts of the proviso. There is no contradiction.
9. **Dangerous Drugs Act, relaxation order schedule.**
   - The substance names agree. The per-substance preparation counts differ (opium 30 against 31, morphine 3 against 2, cocaine 6 against 5) because the two readings counted cross-references differently.
   - The counts are not used in the JSON.
10. **NAFDAC FAQ, "Nationally Controlled Drugs".**
    - Reading 1 left out "Thiopental". Reading 2 included it, and a third reading confirmed it is printed.
    - Settled by readings 2 and 3. Not one of our items.
11. **Indian hemp wording: "separate resin" against "separated resin".** This is not a disagreement between readings. The two words come from different texts: the Dangerous Drugs Act s. 2 and the Indian Hemp Act s. 1.

No other row differed. The Yellow List names, the Indian Hemp Act, the version lines and the NDLEA post all matched exactly.

## 7. Unsettled

These points are also in the JSON.

1. **Source of the Dangerous Drugs Act and the Indian Hemp Act.**
   - No government-hosted text was found, so both were read in PLAC, and the Indian Hemp Act also in FAOLEX.
   - The records taken from them are draft even though the two readings agree.
   - **The editor must decide** whether to accept these copies.
2. **NDLEA Act s. 52 refers to the UN schedules without a date.**
   - If the reference is fixed, substances the UN scheduled after the Act's text are outside it. The enactment line ends with 1999 No. 62.
   - This affects all 35 by-reference items, and the by-reference part of the named ones.
   - The dates of UN scheduling were not read.
3. **Codeine is outside the NDLEA Act definitions.**
   - s. 52 names only the First Schedule of the 1961 Convention, and the UN layer places codeine in Schedule II.
   - Its only Nigerian listing is the Dangerous Drugs Act order L.N. 83 of 1955, and that text is the PLAC copy.
4. **NAFDAC's full "List of controlled Drugs" has no link, and the FAQ gives examples only.**
   - Items absent from the FAQ may still be on NAFDAC's list. Examples are esketamine, tapentadol, GBL, kratom and the newer benzodiazepines.
5. **Esketamine.** NAFDAC names ketamine, but the page has no isomer clause.
6. **Cannabidiol.** Whether CBD products made from the plant fall under the "Indian hemp" definitions, or under s. 8(1)(b) (extracts and tinctures), is a question of interpretation.
7. **Dangerous Drugs Act s. 9(1).** Whether this clause on post-1931 products of opium or coca alkaloids reaches any item that is not named (buprenorphine, for example, if it is such a product) was not decided.
