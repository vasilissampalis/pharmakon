## 2. How Ethiopian law lists these drugs

### Proclamation No. 1112/2019, Art. 2 (definitions)

**Art. 2(11), narcotic drugs.**
- English: "a medicine subject to control in accordance with the Convention issued by United Nations" and ratified by Ethiopia. It also includes "a drug that is categorized as narcotic drug by the executive organ".
- Amharic: the term is "ናርኮቲክ መድኃኒት" (narcotic medicine). The control is under "የናርኮቲክ መድኃኒቶች ስምምነት" (the narcotic drugs convention).

**Art. 2(12), psychotropic substance.**
- English: "any substance subject to control in accordance with the Convention issued by United Nations" and ratified by Ethiopia. It also includes a drug the executive organ categorizes as psychotropic.
- Amharic: the term is "ሳይኮትሮፒክ መድኃኒት" (psychotropic medicine). The control is under "የሳይኮትሮፒክ ንጥረ-ነገሮች ስምምነት" (the psychotropic substances convention), and the thing controlled is a "ንጥረ-ነገር" (substance).

**Other points in Art. 2.**
- "Convention" has no definition of its own.
- Art. 2(9) defines "medicine" by its use in diagnosis, treatment, mitigation or prevention.
- Art. 2(57): the "executive organ" is the federal body that administers the Proclamation. The tramadol notice shows that this body is EFDA.
- Neither definition gives a date, a schedule number, or "as amended" wording.

**Effect.** Every substance in the 1961 or 1971 schedules is covered by reference, and so is anything EFDA categorizes. Section 7 sets out the open questions.

### Directive No. 1121/2025

- Art. 2(6) applies the Proclamation's definitions to the Directive.
- Art. 3 makes it apply to production, import, export, distribution, storage, transport, prescribing, dispensing and use.
- Art. 7(6) refers to "narcotic drugs classified in Schedule IV of the 1961 Convention".
- The readable text names no substance and has no list.

### The National List (May 2017)

**Part I: psychotropic substances.** The Purpose says Part 1 covers "Schedules I – IV" of the 1971 Convention, but only two tables are printed, and they have no item numbers:
- "Psychotropic Substances in Schedule III" (2 entries): PENTAZOCINE, PENTOBARBITAL.
- "Psychotropic Substances in Schedule IV" (13 entries): ALPRAZOLAM, BROMAZEPAM, CHLORDIAZEPOXIDE, CLONAZEPAM, DIAZEPAM, FLURAZEPAM, LORAZEPAM, MEDAZEPAM, MIDAZOLAM, OXAZEPAM, PHENOBARBITAL, TEMAZEPAM, ZOLPIDEM.

**Part II: narcotic drugs.** The Purpose says Part 2 is "subdivided into three sections", but only two are printed:
- Section 1, "Drugs included in schedule I of the 1961 convention" (6 entries): COCAINE, DIPHENOXYLATE, FENTANYL, METHADONE, MORPHINE, PETHIDINE.
- Section 2, "Drugs included in schedule II of the 1961 convention" (1 entry): CODEINE.

**Parts III and IV.** These give the pure-drug content of salts (15 and 8 rows). They name no substance beyond Parts I and II; Part IV spells codeine "Codiene".

**Annexes.**
- Annex 1 (the first one so numbered): health institutions authorized to handle narcotic and psychotropic prescriptions.
- Annex 1, form NPS/01/A: pre-prints 9 narcotic names.
- Annex 2, form NPS/01/B: pre-prints 19 psychotropic names. Two of them are not in Part I: "Methylphenidate" and "Dextroamphetamine". It also prints "Aprazole" and "Medazolam".
- The other forms (NPS/02/A to NPS/16/B and NPS/14) pre-print no names.

### The tramadol notice

- It quotes the Proclamation's definition of narcotic drug and concludes "hence the Tramadol HCI is categorized as Narcotic drugs".
- Amharic: "ትራማዶል በናርኮቲክ መድኃኒትነት ተመድቧል፡፡" (tramadol has been classified as a narcotic medicine).
- It also states "Tramadol HCl is a prescription Opioid Pain killer for moderate pain." This is recorded as `medical_use`, the only one in the file.

## 4. Method

**Readings.** Every chunk was read twice through WebFetch, with differently worded prompts and output formats, and the two readings were compared by inline Python scripts.

- **National List** (23,733 characters, so each call held the whole text):
  - cover, contents and Purpose;
  - Parts I and II (22 rows);
  - Parts III and IV, names and salts (23 rows);
  - names in the Annex forms (9 + 19);
  - the Introduction sentence;
  - a check that Part II has no third section (2 readings).
- **Proclamation:**
  - English Art. 2(11) and (12): readings 1 and 3, because reading 2 paraphrased;
  - Art. 2(9) and the masthead: 2 readings;
  - Amharic Art. 2(11) and (12): 2 readings, after one paraphrased attempt;
  - Art. 2(57) and Art. 2(14): reading 1, with reading 2's excerpts matching.
- **Directive 1121:** four readings covering the version line, Arts. 1-3, Art. 7(6) and the end of the text.
- **Tramadol notice:** notice page and PDF, 2 readings each of the decisive sentences, the medical-use sentence and the Amharic clause.
- **UN dexamfetamine row:** 2 readings.

**Matching.**
- A script matched the 77 items against all 86 names and descriptions read, using name variants as keys and whole-word matching for short keys. The 86 are the Part I-IV names, the Part II chemical names, the Annex form names, the notice's two spellings and the Introduction's five phrases.
- I checked every hit by eye. 16 items had hits; four hits were rejected:
  - **Amfetamine** from "Dextroamphetamine": the form names only the dextro isomer.
  - **Cannabis and coca leaf** from the Introduction ("coca and its derivatives, cannabis"): this is running text, not a list entry.
  - **Morphine** from "3-methylmorphine": this is codeine's chemical name. Morphine is still matched by its own entry.
- UN placements and treaty dates were read by script from the site's data.

## 6. Disagreements between readings, and how each was settled

1. **Proclamation, whether "Convention" is defined.**
   - Reading 1: no separate definition. Reading 2 paraphrased and answered "CONVENTION DEFINED: yes", meaning that the definitions refer to conventions.
   - A third reading found "No separate definition". Settled by readings 1 and 3.
2. **Proclamation, Art. 2(11) and (12) in English.** Reading 2 paraphrased, keeping only short excerpts. Reading 3, taken in short excerpts, is word-for-word identical to reading 1, and reading 2's excerpts occur inside it.
3. **Proclamation, Art. 2(9).** The only difference is a comma: reading 1 has "disease disorder" and the check reading has "disease, disorder". This is not quoted in the JSON.
4. **Proclamation, Amharic Art. 2(11) and (12).** The first second-reading paraphrased. A re-read in four short quotations is identical to reading 1.
5. **National List, Parts III and IV.**
   - The first attempt was refused, because the summarizer will not copy whole tables. Names and salts were then read twice, and all 23 rows are identical.
   - Both readings attribute the unnamed second "Morphine" row (Hydrochloride) to morphine.
   - The Part III heading was given short in reading 1 and in full in reading 2 ("... under international control"). This is a format difference only.
6. **Tramadol notice, PDF.**
   - The first PDF reading would not copy the notice line by line. A short-excerpt reading followed.
   - The decisive sentence is identical to the notice page's reading once spaces are ignored. The PDF's text layer drops spaces ("As perProclamation", "subjectto").
7. **Directive 1121, length.** The first reading called the text "cut off" and estimated 60,000 characters. The offset readings fix it at 50,102 characters, ending "ማንኛውም ናርኮቲክ ወ" in Art. 26(2), and the two end-of-text readings agree.
   - **Two numbering systems in the Directive.** It prints itself as 1121/2018 in Amharic and 1121/2025 in English, and cites the Proclamation as 1112/2011 in Art. 2(5) and 1112/2019 in the English Preamble. This is the Ethiopian and Gregorian calendars inside the document, not a disagreement between readings.

**No other row differed:** Parts I-II (22 rows), the front matter, the Annex names (9 + 19 + the institution types), the Introduction sentence, the masthead, the Directive's facts and the UN dexamfetamine row all matched.

## 7. Unsettled

These points are also in the JSON.

1. **Currency of the National List.**
   - The list read is dated May 2017.
   - Directive No. 1121/2025 cannot be read after Art. 26(2), including any annexes, and the "New NPS Directive" (September 2026) has no text layer.
   - The tramadol notice shows the "NPS List" being added to, but no consolidated current list was found.
   - All "Not found" records are therefore draft. Ketamine, esketamine and tapentadol, for example, may be on a newer list.
   - **Needed from Vasilis:** saved PDFs of Directive No. 1121/2025 (full) and of the "New NPS Directive". A reader could then check their annexes.
2. **Coverage by reference.**
   - Art. 2(11) and (12) refer to the UN conventions with no date and no schedules.
   - Whether the reference reaches substances scheduled internationally after 28 February 2019 is not decided, and the UN scheduling dates were not read.
   - This affects all 41 "by reference" items. **The editor must decide** whether to keep these records or to show only named listings.
3. **"A medicine" in Art. 2(11).**
   - Whether non-medicinal 1961-Convention drugs fall within the definition is a question of interpretation. Examples are heroin, cannabis, coca leaf and the fentanyl analogues.
   - Art. 2(12) says "any substance", so the question does not arise for 1971-Convention items.
4. **Methylphenidate and dexamfetamine.**
   - Both are named only in the import form NPS/01/B, not in Part I. Part I prints only Schedules III and IV.
   - **The editor must decide** whether the form counts as listing.
   - Dexamfetamine is matched from "Dextroamphetamine" by name.
5. **Tramadol.** Its categorization comes from an undated notice (filed under /2022/10/). No list naming tramadol was found.
6. **Proclamation No. 1112/2019 beyond Art. 11.** It was not read in either government copy: any provisions on lists, repeal or transition are unknown.
7. **Precursor Directive No. 393/2012 E.C.** It is illegible, so any national precursor list it may contain (relevant to GBL and 1,4-butanediol) was not checked.
8. **Cannabidiol.** Whether CBD products made from cannabis fall under cannabis's by-reference coverage is a question of interpretation.
