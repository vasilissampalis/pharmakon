Files: `data/claims/_incoming/pdf1114_w681.json` (three new draft claims, schema-valid), `681_simple.json` (plain lines,

## cl_xc_psychedelic_beliefs: very low, replicated by the same team

Timmermann et al. (2021): 122 of 819–866 ceremony attendees with paired data shifted by d = 0.33 (4 weeks) and 0.46
(6 months); the sample already leaned non-physicalist; conversions ran both ways; group synchrony, not mystical or visual
scores, predicted the shift (exploratory). Its trial: 59 patients, p = 0.041 one-tailed (0.082 two-sided, my
calculation), escitalopram d = 0.2. Nayak & Griffiths (2022): 1,606 people recruited for a belief change, "before" recalled
8.2 years later. Low, −2 risk of bias. Nayak is not counted as independent replication, since recruitment builds in the
direction; the editor may prefer "replicated". Both teams say their data cannot judge truth; the claim says so.
Missing: Timmermann's supplement, Kettner et al. (2021) and the trial report (Carhart-Harris et al., 2021, NEJM 384,
1402–1411: blinding, registered outcomes); Nayak's supplement. No DOIs printed.

## cl_psi_personality: very low, contested (debate box)

MacLean et al. (2011): +2.8 T in 52 volunteers (p = 0.023), only in the complete-mystical subgroup, whose 16-month result
is p = 0.0503; I count 17 of 51 visible points below zero in Figure 1, and about +1.3 without the three largest rises
(approximate). Erritzoe et al. (2018): 19 patients, Openness +4.9, Neuroticism −5.7, Extraversion +6.5 at 3 months, with the
authors' state-versus-trait caveat; Openness not linked to the experience, unlike MacLean. I added three anchors from the
checked card `sc_griffiths_2006` (PDF not on file): the 2006 randomized comparison (15 against 15) found no personality
difference (no values), which MacLean does not mention, and observers' behaviour ratings were never linked to personality
scores. **Editor: confirm this use.** Low (before-after estimates), −2 risk of bias, −1 each inconsistency, indirectness,
imprecision. No paper checked a change against behaviour. Agrees with the mystical-experience answer (no NEO change in 2006).
Missing: Griffiths et al. (2011, Study 2, cited in press); the 2006 PDF (record DOI 10.1007/s00213-006-0457-5); Schmid &
Liechti (2018, Psychopharmacology 235, 535–545, an LSD null as Erritzoe describes it); the patient trial reports.

## cl_xc_visual_patterns: very low, single study

Bressloff et al. (2001) as a model: Klüver's four classes, the logarithmic retina-to-V1 map, V1 wiring from cats, macaques,
owl monkeys and tree shrews, the drug as an excitability setting; limits as briefed, plus the unsourced 30–40 repetitions
and images the authors say the model cannot explain. Low, −1 risk of bias, −1 indirectness. Missing: Klüver (1966),
Ermentrout & Cowan (1979), Bressloff et al. (2000b), any imaging test in people on a drug. No DOIs printed.

## Cards and records against the PDFs

- `sc_timmermann_2021` (comparison.controls) gives both trial arms psychological support; the PDF does not (p. 9: two
  25 mg doses plus daily placebo, or two 1 mg doses plus daily escitalopram). I followed the PDF.
- `sc_bressloff_2001` says the mechanism is applied to flickering light, eye pressure, anaesthetics and sleep onset; the PDF
  names hallucinogens or flickering lights (p. 299) and uses a pressure phosphene only as a compared drawing (p. 317).
- MacLean et al. call every high-dose session blinded, but by its card the 2006 trial had six open-label psilocybin
  sessions; MacLean's 35 Study 1 volunteers include at least five of them (my inference).
- All other numbers used match the cards; the five reference records match the PDFs.
