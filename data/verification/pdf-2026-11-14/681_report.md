## cl_xc_psychedelic_beliefs: very low, replicated by the same team

Timmermann et al. (2021): 122 of 819–866 ceremony attendees shifted by d = 0.33 (4 weeks) and 0.46 (6 months), already
leaning that way; conversions ran both ways; group synchrony, not mystical or visual scores, predicted the shift
(exploratory). Trial: 59 patients, p = 0.041 one-tailed (0.082 two-sided, my calculation), escitalopram d = 0.2. Nayak &
Griffiths (2022): 1,606 recruited for belief change, "before" recalled 8.2 years later. Low, −2 risk of bias. Nayak is not
counted as replication (recruitment builds in the direction); the editor may prefer "replicated". Truth: both teams say
their data cannot judge it. Missing: Timmermann's supplement, Kettner et al. (2021), the trial report (Carhart-Harris et
al., 2021, NEJM 384, 1402–1411), Nayak's supplement. No DOIs printed.

## cl_psi_personality: very low, contested (debate box)

MacLean et al. (2011): +2.8 T in 52 volunteers (p = 0.023), only in the complete-mystical subgroup, whose 16-month result is
p = 0.0503; I count 17 of 51 visible points below zero (Figure 1), about +1.3 without the three largest rises. Erritzoe et
al. (2018): 19 patients, Openness +4.9, Neuroticism −5.7, Extraversion +6.5 at 3 months; state-versus-trait caveat; Openness
not linked to the experience, unlike MacLean. **Editor: confirm** three anchors from the checked card `sc_griffiths_2006`
(PDF not on file): its randomized comparison (15 against 15) found no personality difference (no values), unmentioned by
MacLean; observers' behaviour ratings were never linked to personality. Low (before-after estimates), −2 risk of bias, −1
each inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision. Nobody checked a change against behaviour. Consistent with the
mystical-experience answer. Missing: Griffiths et al. (2011, in press when cited); the 2006 PDF (record DOI
10.1007/s00213-006-0457-5); Schmid & Liechti (2018, Psychopharmacology 235, 535–545, an LSD null as described); the patient
trial reports.

## cl_xc_visual_patterns: very low, single study

Bressloff et al. (2001) as a model: Klüver's four classes, the logarithmic retina-to-V1 map, animal V1 wiring, the drug as an
excitability setting; limits as briefed, plus the unsourced 30–40 repetitions and images the model cannot explain. Low, −1
risk of bias, −1 indirectness. Missing: Klüver (1966), Ermentrout & Cowan (1979), Bressloff et al. (2000b), any imaging
test in people on a drug. No DOIs printed.

## Cards against the PDFs

- `sc_timmermann_2021` gives both trial arms psychological support; the PDF does not (p. 9). I followed the PDF.
- `sc_bressloff_2001` says the mechanism is applied to flickering light, eye pressure, anaesthetics and sleep onset; the PDF
  names hallucinogens or flickering lights (p. 299), eye pressure only as a compared drawing (p. 317).
- MacLean et al. call every high-dose session blinded, but by its card the 2006 trial had six open-label psilocybin
  sessions; MacLean's 35 Study 1 volunteers include at least five (my inference).
- Otherwise cards and the five reference records match the PDFs.
